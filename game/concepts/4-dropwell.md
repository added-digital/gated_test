# Dropwell

*Angle: start from scalability.* You keep a tea house on a hill above a dry well where everything anyone has ever lost ends up; the people who lost things wait on your veranda while you climb down with a lantern and bring their things back up.

**The player climbs down the well to bring up what a guest upstairs has lost.**

## The surface

The home is the sakura tea house: tatami room, engawa veranda, stone garden, pond, the 3 m bridge, bamboo, lanterns, and in the middle of the stone garden a stone well with a windlass. Mornings are slow: petals, pond, Oba on the engawa with her tea, Pim pretending he does not live here. The gate bell rings and someone is at the gate having lost something; they come in, sit, and wait, because you are the one who goes down. At dusk you lay the haul out on the engawa: claimed things go into a guest's hands, everything else goes to the workbench, where lost rope is knotted into your rope and lost oil fills your lantern. Guests who get their thing back sign the Found Book, come back, bring others, and some ask to stay; the house grows a room at a time around whoever is in it. There is no clock on the surface; its pace is walking, pouring, and two or three conversations with people who remember what you said yesterday.

## The underground

You tie on at the wellhead, light the lantern, and climb down a dry stone shaft; the lantern's light is the only world you can see and it shrinks as the oil burns. Ledges open off the shaft every ten metres, each a cave the size of your garden, strewn with what the world dropped recently: coins, a left shoe, an umbrella, a ladder, a ring in a drip, a nest of socks that moves. You grab what fits in the satchel, solve what blocks you (a lost ladder reaches a lost key; a lost key opens a lost door; the Keychain, a centipede of keys, can be led to a lock), and keep moving so the Sockrat does not take your lightest thing. Below the rope's end there is always a glint, and the Hum, a lost tune, is always one ledge further down than your rope reaches. Things sink: what you saw at 10 m last week is at 14 m now, so what you did not grab is going away, and what you dropped will be deeper next time. Each visit the ledges keep their shape and landmarks but the loose things re-roll from the pool for that depth band, something new falls past you on the way down and lands somewhere, and the drips and drafts move. You end the trip by ringing the bell at the rope (hauled up with everything) or by running the oil down to the dark (keep one thing).

## The threshold

The well in the stone garden, across the pond bridge from the house. Crossing is a three-beat gesture you do every time: tie on at the windlass, hold to light the lantern (the light blooms, the music drops out, only the Hum remains), step off the lip. Coming back is the bell: ring it at the rope and Oba hauls you up with everything, any time, free, but it ends the trip; or the dark hauls you up with one thing. One trip per day: stepping off is noon and the haul-up is dusk whatever the oil said. The surface has no clock and the well has nothing else; neither state leaks into the other, which is what makes stepping off deliberate.

## Three dials

- LANTERN, how long: oil burns for 90 s at the start and the lit radius shrinks with it, so the dial is the field of view, not a bar. Track: Wick (slower burn) -> Reservoir (+50% oil) -> Spare Lantern (a second jar that lights when the first dies, half radius) -> the Lost Noon (a late find: a pocket of daylight you can carry once). Built from oil + glass parts.
- ROPE, how far: 10 m at the start, one ledge; ledges every 10 m, older and stranger with depth. Track: knotted from lost cord, 10 -> 20 -> 30 -> 50 -> 80 m; then Tove's windlass (the descent costs half the oil) and the Side Rope (reach a ledge's far shelf). Built from cord parts.
- SATCHEL, how much: 4 slots; a coin is 1, an umbrella 2, a ladder 3, a thimble 1, and a ship does not fit. Track: Patches (+2 slots) -> Frame (one bulky thing on your back) -> Basket (send a load up the rope mid-trip without you, the first mid-trip deposit) -> Pockets (coins stop taking slots). Built from cloth + cord. Dave's harpoon slot is the TOOL, what you can take: Reach (tongs, for far and hot) -> Net (skittish things: nerves, tunes) -> Gloves (heavy), each one arriving as a guest's request.

## The fail state is a bet

When the oil runs out the light collapses to your hands and the game asks one thing: which one do you hold. Everything else in the satchel falls where you stand and sinks one band; it is not destroyed, it is lost again, findable later and deeper. You wake on the engawa at dawn; Oba hauled the rope and does not say how. The bet is live every trip because there is always a glint one ledge down: at 15% oil you can ring now and keep a full satchel of scrap, or go down and come back with only the one thing that matters, held in your hand. Both are wins of different sizes. Nobody dies in Dropwell; things get lost, which is the one thing the well is good at.

## Conversion and two sinks

The haul comes up on the rope and is laid out on the engawa at dusk. Sink 1, RETURN: a thing with an owner goes into that guest's hands; it writes a line in the Found Book, triggers the guest's memory line, and can turn a guest into a resident. Sink 2, SCRAP: everything unclaimed goes to the workbench bin and becomes parts by material (cord, cloth, oil, glass, metal, wood), which the three dials and the tool are built from; coins are the only money and buy from Tove's cart. Nothing wastes: duplicates scrap, and parts beyond what the dials need go into the house (oil lights the path lanterns, cloth makes cushions, wood makes a bench), so over-collecting always makes something nicer. Claimed things are flagged by the Book and cannot be scrapped by accident.

## The second progression

The Found Book on the engawa. Only a return writes a line, and lines cannot be bought. Lines are reputation: at 3, word spreads and a second guest comes; at 6, Tove's cart routes past; at 10, Pim asks for a room and the second tatami room is built; at 15, the bath; at 25, the lantern path and the gate stays open at dusk; at 40, the bottom chapter opens. At each threshold one guest with a line may ask to stay, and residents decide what the house becomes: Pim's room, Tove's workshop replacing the grey bench, Halvar's woodshed, a resident's lost cat in the garden. The house is built out of who you brought something back for.

## What breaks the calm

The gate bell. Someone is standing at the gate holding nothing, and the nothing has a name: Pim's key, Halvar's nerve, Oba's thimble. They walk in, sit on your veranda, and cannot go home, and they stay there, visible, until you go down. The second break, smaller and daily, is the sinking: the thing you saw is going away. The third, scheduled, is the Settling: the well shifts and the known ledges reconnect.

## Where accomplishment lands

On the engawa at dusk, in the handover: the guest takes the thing from your hands, says a line that quotes something you said to them earlier, and the Found Book writes a line. Then the gate is quiet, the petals fall, and the quiet is the reward; the well never congratulates you and neither does Oba. The accomplishment is the house being whole again with one more person in it.

## The one-map conceit

Everything that is lost, anywhere, by anyone, ends up in this one well: objects first, then animals, sounds, places, feelings. A thing arrives at the depth of how long it has been lost and sinks as it is forgotten. So content is one table of Lost Things, each a row with a depth band, weight in slots, pickup kind (grab, tongs, net, gloves), material, a behaviour from a short list (sits, drifts, flees, steals, follows), and an optional owner; the ledges draw from the pool for their band every trip. Adding a creature is a row that moves; adding a guest is a row with a face upstairs and two memory lines; adding an era is a deeper band and one ledge scene; adding a puzzle is two rows that want each other. Nothing new is ever a new system; it is a new thing somebody lost.

## Characters and requests

OBA, 70s, tiny, your predecessor as keeper, retired to the engawa and the windlass. Wants never to go down again and never to be asked why. Introduces the ROPE and the haul-up (dial 2 and the fail-state): "Ten metres, that's what I'll give you. Ring when you want up. When the light goes, one hand." Her own loss, a thimble from forty years ago, is the toy's last beat. PIM, the first guest, exact and anxious, has lost his front-door key and has been sleeping on Oba's cushion for three days. Wants his key, then his umbrella, then a room. Introduces the RETURN (sink 1) and the FOUND BOOK, and he is the memory: he quotes what you said at the gate. TOVE, a tinker, arrives on a cart at Book 6 from somewhere north; wants anything with a mechanism and pays in parts. Introduces SCRAP (sink 2) and the WORKBENCH as a proper place, plus the windlass and basket upgrades: "Lost rope is still rope." HALVAR, big and quiet, arrives at the end of the slice having lost his nerve; it is a skittish thing on ledge 3 that flees the light. Introduces the NET (the tool track) and the first non-object loss, opening the abstract category: tunes, hours, tempers.

## Two lines

- Oba: "Light it before you step off. I won't say that twice. I'm not really saying it once; I'm saying it to the pond."
- Oba: "Tuesday you told me you don't lose things. Tonight you came up holding a spoon. I've put the spoon in the book under Found. I've put you under Lost."

## Later, in order

- The Net (Halvar's nerve): skittish lost things that flee the light; the tool track opens; the first abstract losses (nerves, tunes, tempers). Delivered at the gate by Halvar at Book 8.
- The Thread (Ulla the knitter's lost yarn): lay a thread behind you and follow it back in the dark; ledges get long enough to need it; the bet gains a second variable. Book 12.
- The Old Lost, rope 30 to 50 m: things lost before anyone alive (letters, swords, a whole ship as a fixed landmark) and Captain Brask, who lost the ship forty years ago and has not thought about how to carry it up. Book 15, with the bath.
- The Settling (Oba): the well shifts on a schedule; known ledges reconnect and a sunk band comes within reach for one day; Dave's shifting terrain, scheduled rather than random.
- Residents and rooms (Pim: "I'm not a guest anymore. I'm a feature."): guests with lines ask to stay and the house grows rooms from kit pieces; the Dusk opens, a two-minute bustle of small wants when the gate crowd arrives, the Help toy at the opposite pace to the well; nothing cooked, nobody pays.
- Lost places, rope 80 m: a lost summer (no oil burns there), a lost hour (time stops for a minute), a lost village as a ledge with its own guests who come up with you.
- The Wish Jar (Fru Sol, who reads coins): coins carry wishes; a wish brings one sunk thing up a band; a late soft fix for the bet and the only thing coins are finally for.
- The Bottom: the Hum, the lost tune you heard on the first descent, whose owner is upstairs; the macro arc's end and Oba's reason.

## Presentation

Low-poly 3D in Godot 4.6 with a fixed three-quarter camera (about 35 degrees down, narrow FOV, yaw locked, A Short Hike's reading) on the surface, and the same camera tilted ten degrees steeper in the well, where the lantern's OmniLight is the entire visible world against a black ambient. Why 3D: the kit and the walkable tea house are already glTF in Godot, Claude's scripted-mesh pipeline makes the long tail of props cheap, verticality (a shaft, ledges, a glint below the rope's end) reads physically in 3D and not in 2D, and darkness is free art direction for the underground: a lit radius on grey stone needs no second kit. Not 2D: it throws away the kit and the shaft. Not 2.5D sprites: the prop pipeline is already 3D.

## The kit

REUSED as-is: all 27 pieces for the surface (tatami, shoji, engawa, eaves, stone garden, pond, bridge, bamboo, lanterns, both sakura, props), the walkable scene, and the Remember, Help and Far sound toys as code (dialogue memory, the Dusk bustle, the Hum). ADAPTED: the stone garden re-centred on a wellhead; lanterns get a lit/unlit state; the engawa gets cushions and the Book; the style contract gets a "below" addendum (three dark stone values, one warm lantern value, validator rule unchanged). NEW: wellhead + windlass + rope (half a day), gate + bell (half a day), workbench + Tove's cart (one day), shaft wall segment + ledge floor and wall modules (one day), and the lost-thing props, which are the long tail: about ten minutes each through the existing scripted pipeline, and zero for the grey-box toy (labelled cubes). DROPPED: nothing. Cost: three asset days alongside the code days, and a permanent rule that a lost thing costs one prop, one table row and two lines or it does not go in.

## The first ten minutes

0:00 You wake on the tatami; morning; petals through the shoji. The gate bell rings, far away (the Far sound toy's chime). You walk out onto the engawa, across the bridge, past the well: sixty seconds of calm with nothing to do. 1:00 Pim at the gate holding a cup nobody gave him: "I've lost my key. I'm told you go down." You pick a reply (Remember toy): "probably", "yes", or "who told you". He sits on Oba's cushion. The calm is broken: a stranger on your veranda who cannot go home. 1:45 Oba at the wellhead on a stool. Three lines, no tutorial: ten metres of rope, ring the bell to come up, when the light goes one hand. You hold to light the lantern (the radius blooms, the music drops out). You step off. 2:30 Descent, hand over hand; a sock falls past you, slowly. The Hum, somewhere below. Ledge 1 at 10 m; you step off; the rope swings and ends; below it, dark, and in the dark a glint. 3:00 Ledge 1, lit radius about five metres: four coins, a sock, an umbrella (two slots), a spoon, a shoe, a ladder lying down, and the key on a shelf you cannot reach. First puzzle: drag the ladder, climb, take the key. The Sockrat's nest rustles; you stand still too long and it takes a coin. The radius is visibly smaller than when you landed. 5:30 Satchel full (key, umbrella, spoon, coin), oil at 35%, the glint still below the rope. Back to the rope. Ring. 6:30 Hauled up; dusk. Oba: "Anything?" You cross the bridge; Pim is asleep on the cushion. You lay the haul out and hand him the key. His line quotes your reply from the gate ("You said probably. I have been sitting here with probably."). He signs the Found Book: line one. The gate is quiet; the calm is back; he stays for tea anyway. 8:00 Morning 2. Oba points at the pile: spoon is metal, sock is cloth, coins are coins. The workbench shows the rope upgrade at four cord; you have none. Pim at the gate: "Also my umbrella. Not that one." The glint below the rope's end is the whole hook. 9:00 You tie on and step off again, looking for cord: a bootlace, a ribbon, a lost rope. This is the trip the toy exists to watch.

## The loop toy

Contents: the existing tea house and garden plus one grey wellhead, one grey workbench and the Found Book as a text panel; a shaft and two ledges (10 m and 20 m) in grey boxes; 12 lost things as labelled cubes (coins, sock, umbrella, spoon, shoe, ladder, key, ring, bootlace, ribbon, a lost rope, Oba's thimble), 3 of them claimed (Pim's key at 10 m, Pim's umbrella and Oba's thimble at 20 m); 2 characters (Oba, Pim) with 12 lines each, 4 of them memory lines; 1 creature (the Sockrat: takes your lightest item if you stand still three seconds near its nest); 3 upgrades (Rope 10 to 20 m for four cord, Lantern +50% for two oil, Satchel 4 to 6 slots for three cloth); 2 sinks (hand over on the engawa, scrap at the bench); 1 puzzle (ladder to key); 1 fail-state (the dark: keep one); rooms: tea room, engawa and garden, shaft, two ledges, five in all. Claude build: six days (shaft and rope descent 1; ledges plus the pool spawner 1; lantern radius, satchel and the dark bet 1; return, Book, scrap and upgrades 1; Oba and Pim dialogue with memory 1; tuning 1), with the designer playing at day 3 (first descent) and day 6 (full loop). The single question twenty minutes answers: does seeing a lost thing just beyond the rope's end make the player come up, spend scrap on rope, and go back down without being asked to.

## Hypothesis line

I believe climb-down, grab, ring-up, hand-over with grey boxes holds a confidant 20 minutes because every trip ends with a named person holding what they lost and quoting what you said, and every trip shows one thing just past the rope's end; dead if not by 2026-11-06.

## Shared with Dave, and yours

Shared on purpose, because it is the structure that works: a calm home and a risky out separated by a deliberate threshold; three dials on one trip with one upgrade track each; a fail-state that is a bet (keep one); two sinks so nothing wastes; one non-money progression that decides what the home becomes; a one-map conceit; new systems delivered as requests from named characters at scheduled story moments. Ours: the out is dry and vertical, climbed rather than swum; the constraint is light, and the light is literally your field of view, so the dial is read on the world and not on a bar; the haul is other people's things, so the conversion is a handover and a remembered line rather than a sale, and the nameless-customer problem Hwang regrets is the core instead of a regret; there is no combat and no selling, the risk is dark, sinking and theft; the fail-state returns your haul to the pool, deeper, not to nothing; the content unit, a thing someone lost, is at once an item, a joke, a puzzle piece and a character hook, so one row of data serves wonder, humour and small detail together; and the home grows by who stays, not by what you buy.

## Three ways it fails

- Errand drift: if the only things worth bringing up are the ones a guest asked for, trips become fetch quests and the designer's chore alarm goes off. Guard: at least 70% of any ledge is unclaimed and worth taking (scrap, house, jokes), and the glint below the rope is never a request.
- The dark is a drag: a shrinking light radius can read as frustrating rather than tense, and if a confidant stops going down after the second trip the pacing is wrong. Guard: tune oil to two to three minutes, make the bet cheap and frequent, and let relost things be found again one band deeper.
- The long tail costs too much: the promise is add a lost thing forever, and it breaks the day a lost thing costs more than thirty minutes (one prop, one row, two lines). Guard: the pipeline is enforced from the grey-box toy onward, and any lost thing that needs new code is a new system and arrives through a character request, not through the table.

## The judges

**Designer, 6.5/10.** On paper the most scalable (one Lost Things table: depth band, weight, pickup kind, material, behaviour, owner), with the best rule nobody else has (things sink as they are forgotten, so what you saw at 10 m is at 14 m next week and the bet is a running one), the best voice in the batch (Oba: 'I've put you under Lost'), and real small-wonder creatures (the Sockrat that steals your lightest thing, the Keychain centipede you lead to a lock). But it is nearly the same game as What Fell, the well is a grey shaft with no place-ness or era identity you can see, Nordic names on a Japanese kit leave it unplaced, and the designer would recognise the template under the charm.

*Best idea in it:* Lost things sink as they are forgotten: what you did not take is going away and what you dropped is deeper next time, so the bet never resets and content retires itself.

*The one change:* Give it a place and a visible past: commit to the kit's Japan (or swap the kit and go Nordic) and give each depth band an era you can see on the ledge (last week's keys, a Showa platform, an Edo inn), so depth is time the eye can read and the shaft stops being a grey tube.

**Producer, 7/10.** The most producer-disciplined scaling plan: one Lost Things table (depth band, slots, pickup kind, material, behaviour, owner), a standing rule that a lost thing costs one prop, one row and two lines or does not ship, and the sinking mechanic, which creates urgency without a clock, turns the fail-state into a deferred loss, and is the reason to buy rope, so the hypothesis tests exactly the item-to-upgrade-to-reach loop the designer loves in Dave. Kit as-is, three asset days, the three existing toys reused as code, and Oba's lines are the best writing in the set. The home is the weakness: no surface clock, one trip per day pinned to noon and dusk, 90-second oil, and the Dusk bustle deferred to a later stage, so the surface idles between short trips and the risk list never names it (Moonlighter's starved half). Six-day toy with a pool spawner, a creature and a physics ladder puzzle is over the bar.

*Best idea in it:* Things sink as they are forgotten: what you saw at 10 m is at 14 m next week, what you drop lands one band deeper, and the fail-state returns your haul to the pool rather than to nothing, so every trip carries pressure and every loss is a reason to come back.

*The one change:* Pull the Dusk bustle into the toy and drop the rigid one-trip-per-day rule, so the home has a recurring hands-on beat at the opposite pace to the well and the player is never standing on the engawa with nothing to do.
