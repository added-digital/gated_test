# Yamabiko — a tea house on a hollow mountain

*Angle: start from the kit.* You keep a tea house on a mountain pass in Japan; under the garden well the mountain is hollow and everything ever lost in Japan ends up down there, so you climb down with a paper lantern and a belly full of hot tea to bring lost things back up to the people who miss them.

**The player carries lost things up out of the cold to give them back to the people who miss them.**

## The surface

The surface is the kit as it stands: a one-room tea house with a hearth and an iron kettle, the engawa, and a garden with one sakura and a stone well under a wooden lid. Its pace is untimed and warm: you walk, sit, pour, talk, read letters and place things; nothing here can hurt you and nothing needs maintenance. Pim, a small tengu who was on this pass before the house was, lives on the engawa and remembers every word you say; travellers come up the road and sit for a bowl (Sudo the postal runner at dawn, Hatsu the old pilgrim, Enoki the curio dealer with his donkey), each with a name, a want, and a memory of your last conversation. What you do between trips: drink tea at the hearth (that sets your warmth for the next descent), lay your haul out on the tokonoma shelf (the lost-and-found), do the Dawn Run when Sudo bursts in (the one timed burst at home: wrap, label and bag the things that have owners before his tea goes cold), read the thank-you letters that come back up the pass with coins pinned inside, and carry what nobody claims out into the garden. What grows is the kit itself: the pond, the 3 m bridge, the stone garden, the bamboo, the lanterns and the second tatami room are all earned pieces that come up from below one at a time, and the guestbook fills with signatures that turn "a hut at the pass" into "the hanami place". Residents accumulate: Hatsu stays once she has her pipe; an awake kettle-spirit becomes your kettle. The home is where accomplishment is cashed, never where it is earned.

## The underground

Under the well the mountain is hollow: lava-tube chambers, an ice room, a black lake, the sakura's roots for a ceiling, chambers stacked in rings down the shaft, each ring one grade darker and colder than the last. Its pace is tense, quiet and alone: one bar (warmth) draining from the moment you leave the rope, one light (the lantern) deciding what you can see, one count (slots) deciding what you can bring; no combat, no map, no menus; you find your way by sound, which is the Far-sound toy exactly: a bell, a cough, a child counting, always one chamber further than you'd like. You gather lost things: a comb, a child's sandal, a sake cup, a letter with half an address, a bicycle bell, a torii beam; the longer a thing has been lost the more it is worth, and the oldest have woken up as tsukumogami (an umbrella that flaps off like a bat, sandals that run in pairs, a lantern that leads you the wrong way) and must be approached with the right tool and a calm hand; the mountain's own cold things (ice-flowers, spring water) are down here too and cannot exist upstairs. Small things fit the furoshiki; large things need the frame; huge things (the bronze bell) need rope and a reason. Each visit the mountain has breathed: the fog tide has risen or fallen, opening some passages and drowning others; new lost things have drifted in (whatever was lost today, anywhere in Japan); what you dropped last time is somewhere, moved. Every trip ends either at the rope by your choice or numb by the mountain's, and the best thing in reach is always visible in the last fifth of your warmth.

## The threshold

The stone well in the garden, under a wooden lid, three steps from the kettle. Crossing is a ritual of three deliberate acts at the well-head, each setting one dial: drink (the warmth bar fills to what your tea tier allows), light the lantern (its grade decides how deep you can see), shoulder the carry (its slots decide how much). Then "Go down?". Yes is a six-second rope descent that cannot be skipped: the palette slides from warm to cold, the kettle thins into drips, the camera swings under the garden. Below the lid there are no prompts and no menus. The only way up is the rope you came down (deeper rings later have side-wells you open from below, which surface in new corners of the garden, Mystery Dungeon style). The return is the mirror: hands on the rope, lid, light, warmth coming back into the colour, the kettle whistle, and Pim's one line about what you held.

## Three dials

- WARMTH (how long). You carry the hearth down as the tea in your belly; the Hollow's cold drains it; at zero you go numb. Track: tea tier (bancha 4 min, sencha 6, gyokuro 9, matcha 12), then a kairo hand-warmer (ash pouch +1 min, charcoal kairo +2, lacquered kairo +4), then the hanten padded coat (cold drains 25% slower). Tea leaves come up from the Hollow as lost tea tins and from guests; the kairo from Enoki; the hanten is Hatsu's gift.
- LIGHT (how far). Chambers have a darkness grade 1-4; a lantern of grade N shows the floor in grade-N dark; below your grade you see only edges and the cold drains double (fear is cold). Track: candle chochin (grade 1, the kit's own lantern), oil andon (2, Hatsu's), fox-fire lantern (3, lit by the shrine's kitsune), firefly-stone (4, found, not bought: the mountain's own glow). Each grade opens the next ring down.
- CARRY (how much, and how big). Track: furoshiki cloth (2 slots, small only), bamboo basket (4), shoiko pack-frame (6, one large), oni's sack (8, two large or one awake spirit), rope (drag one huge thing, the bell, at half speed). Alongside it the Hand, which is what you can take at all: bare hands, bamboo pole (reach ledges), hand-net (catch flying things), warm cloth (calm an awake thing so it lets you carry it). The Hand is to this game what the harpoon is to Dave.

## The fail state is a bet

Warmth hits zero: you sit down "just for a moment", the screen softens, and you wake on the engawa under a blanket with the kettle whistling and ONE item still in your hands (you choose which, from everything you carried; the rest goes back into the Hollow, moved). Nobody dies, no money is lost, no day is lost. Pim claims he carried you up ("don't ask how"), which is a lie the game pays off much later: the Yamabiko, the mountain's echo, carries you, and that is the game's slow mystery. The bet is built into the level rule: the sound is always one chamber past your warmth, and the bell, the glint or the awake thing is visible in the last fifth of the bar. Go up warm with four small things, or push for the one old thing and come up with one. Pim remembers what you kept and quotes it back.

## Conversion and two sinks

Conversion: lost things go on the tokonoma shelf; at dawn Sudo's bag takes everything that has an owner down the mountain; the next dawn letters come back up with one sentence from a stranger and mon (coins) pinned inside; mon buys the dial upgrades from Sudo (lamp oil, baskets) and Enoki (kairo, frame, sack). Value is by how long a thing was lost: fresh (this year) 10-30 mon, old (decades) 60-150, ancient or awake (a century, a tsukumogami) 300 and up; Enoki appraises. Sink 1, RETURN: hand it over for money, a letter, and, if the owner is a guest on your engawa, a guestbook signature. Sink 2, KEEP: carry it out into the garden or house, where every item has a placed form (a lost comb sits in the alcove; a lost stone lantern becomes a garden lantern; a lost torii beam becomes the torii; an awake kettle-spirit becomes your kettle and adds a minute of warmth; an awake umbrella shades the engawa and makes guests stay longer and say more). Keeping never pays mon; it pays beauty, residents and dialogue. Over-collecting never wastes: if nobody claims it, the garden does, and duplicates of a kind bundle into one bigger letter.

## The second progression

The GUESTBOOK. Every named traveller whose lost thing you return signs it, and tells the next one up the road. Signatures, not money, decide what the tea house becomes: 3 signatures, the pass is marked on the postal map (Sudo starts carrying strangers' things, the stranger-letter economy opens); 7, "a waystation" (the second tatami room, Enoki's monthly climb); 12, "a stop on the pilgrim road" (the shrine up the slope opens, the kitsune, fox-fire); 20, "the hanami place" (the festival, when every signer sits in the garden at once and the bell is raised). The progression is social, Undertale's rule that friendships unlock the route, and it is the direct fix for Hwang's regret: nobody at this home is nameless.

## What breaks the calm

Every dawn, two things, both from the well. First: the mountain breathes out. A cold gust lifts the lid, a sound comes up (the far-sound: a temple bell, a cough, a child counting), and something lost lands in the garden (a sandal, a kite, a letter with half an address). Second: someone comes up the pass road who has lost something, sits on your engawa, and says so with a name attached. That is the whole list: no raids, no weather damage, no hunger, no rot; the house never needs maintenance. The calm comes back only one way: the thing is in the hands it belongs in and the guest walks down the road (or stays). The mountain's exhale is scheduled, so bursts never overlap: there is always one break open, never three.

## Where accomplishment lands

On the climb out, and then the morning after. The climb: hands on the rope, the lid, light, the palette warming, the kettle whistle, and Pim's single line about what you held, the only moment the game comments on your haul. The morning: the thing leaves your hands, into Sudo's bag before his tea goes cold, into Hatsu's lap on the engawa, or onto its spot in the garden where it stays forever; and a dawn later a letter comes back up the pass with a coin and one sentence from someone you will never meet. Accomplishment is designed to land when the thing leaves your hands, not when you pick it up; the garden is the scoreboard you can walk through.

## The one-map conceit

"Everything ever lost in Japan ends up under this mountain, and the longer it has been lost, the more awake it is." One vertical hollow; its rings are places. Because lost PLACES count (a village flooded by a dam, a shrine that burned, a Showa-era train platform, a summer somebody lost), every new zone is one more ring of the same hole; because lost PEOPLE count, new characters climb up the well with a cutscene instead of appearing from a menu; because anything from any era can be lost, every item, creature (any lost thing that woke up) and season is a data entry, an item, a letter and a chamber, with no new systems. Confined hole, small team: Hwang's logic, with a comb instead of a tuna.

## Characters and requests

PIM, a small tengu kid in an oversized monk's hat who was on the pass before the house was; wants the garden to be "the best on the mountain" and for you to stop needing to be carried up; cocky, remembers every word. Introduces the well and the first descent, the warmth dial ("drink first, idiot"), the keep-one fail state (he takes the credit), and the KEEP sink ("put it in the garden, it's ours now"). SUDO, 19, a hikyaku postal runner, always late and docked for it; wants to never be late again; he is the Help toy with a face. Introduces the RETURN sink, the Dawn Run (wrap, label, bag before his tea goes cold), mon and letters, and the first two purchasable upgrades (lamp oil, bamboo basket). HATSU, an old pilgrim on her 33rd circuit of the road; wants her husband's pipe, lost on this pass forty years ago ("I can hear him tapping it down there"); stays as a resident when she gets it. Introduces darkness grades and the LIGHT track (she gives you her oil andon), the Guestbook (first signature), and the fact that lost things have owners who are sitting on your engawa. ENOKI, a curio dealer with a donkey named Minister, climbs the pass monthly; wants "anything older than me". Introduces appraisal (fresh, old, ancient), the CARRY track (frame, sack, rope), awake things, and the Hand. Scheduled, not autonomous: Pim at 0:00; Sudo at the first return; Hatsu after the third return; Enoki at three signatures; the kitsune at twelve.

## Two lines

- Pim: "You said 'back before the kettle boils.' It boiled. Twice. I drank it both times. Don't look at me like that, you weren't here."
- Pim: "You dropped the gold and kept the comb. Don't explain. I've already told the umbrella it was wise, and the umbrella agrees with me about everything."

## Later, in order

- Hatsu and the Light track (feature): darkness grades 2-3, the oil andon, the Guestbook with its first signature, lost things that belong to someone on your engawa; scheduled after the third return.
- Enoki and the Carry track (feature): appraisal grades, basket to frame to sack, the Hand (pole, net, cloth), buying with mon; scheduled at three signatures.
- Awake things as a class (creatures + feature): tsukumogami with behaviours (flee, lead astray, hide, follow), calming with the warm cloth, and KEEPING a spirit as a functional resident (kettle-spirit adds warmth, lantern-spirit lights the garden, umbrella makes guests linger).
- Ring three, the Drowned Village (map): the first lost PLACE, a hamlet flooded by a dam, half under the black lake, with its own lost things and one lost person (Mizue, the ferry girl) who climbs the well as a new character; scheduled at seven signatures, opens the second tatami room.
- The shrine and the kitsune (surface map + feature): up the slope behind the house using the kit's bamboo and a found torii, the fox lights the fox-fire lantern (grade 3), introduces the mountain's trust and the Yamabiko mystery (who really carries you up); scheduled at twelve signatures.
- Ring four, the Lost Summer (map + season): a ring where it is a lost August, hot-spring steam, fireflies, the firefly-stone (grade 4); ice-flowers from the ice room become sellable to summer guests; the surface season changes to match with kit variants (green sakura, cicadas).
- The Hanami festival (event): twenty signatures; every signer in the garden at once; the bronze bell from ring two is raised with rope, frame and four pairs of hands; chapter one ends; the Yamabiko answers back.
- Rings forever (content plan): each later ring is a lost place with its own lost people, things and letters (a burned Edo inn, a Showa train platform, a shuttered school, a lost New Year), shipped as updates with no new systems; three rings at early access, four more to 1.0, Dave's own shape.

## Presentation

Low-poly 3D in Godot 4.6, fixed three-quarter camera (about 45 degrees down, yaw locked per space, snapping per chamber in the Hollow, no free orbit), WASD plus one interact button, no combat. Why 3D: the 27-piece kit is already glTF low-poly 3D and already walkable in Godot, so 2D or 2.5D would throw away the one finished asset and the designer's stated taste (A Short Hike's look). Why this camera: it keeps readability close to Dave's side view while letting the light dial be literally one OmniLight3D with a falloff per grade, so "how far you can see" needs no UI; the darkness is the dial. Vertical chambers mean the camera only ever follows down and up. Everything else is cheap in 4.6: the fog tide is a WorldEnvironment volume height, cold is a colour-grade LUT lerp on the rope, the tsukumogami are kit props with a NavigationAgent3D and a flee state.

## The kit

REUSED as-is: the whole kit is the surface. Tatami room, shoji, engawa, eaves, stone garden, pond, 3 m bridge, bamboo, lanterns, both sakura, props, and the walkable tea-house scene is the home scene on day one. ADAPTED: most of the garden pieces are hidden at the start and become the KEEP sink's rewards; the pond, bridge, stone garden, bamboo and second tree are not placed by a level designer, they come up out of the well as lost things and the player puts them where they go, so the home's whole growth arc costs zero new art. The kit lantern is the grade-1 lantern. DROPPED: nothing. NEW: one prop now (the stone well with a lid, piece 28, under the existing style contract and validator); after the loop toy passes, a cave kit (about 20 pieces: tube walls, ice, roots, lake, rope) and a lost-things kit (about 30 props, a third of which are existing kit props "aged" with a tint and a chip), plus four characters. Cost: half a day for the well; about two weeks of Claude asset work for the cave and lost-things kits, which stay grey until the toy earns them.

## The first ten minutes

0:00 Calm. Dawn at the tea house. Kettle on the hearth, steam, one petal falls. The well lid is on. You can walk, sit, drink; nothing asks anything of you. 1:00 Pim, on the engawa: "You're new. I was here before the house. Don't touch the well." Three replies; Pim stores yours. 2:00 The break. A bell, far down, under the garden (the Far-sound toy). The lid rattles. A paper umbrella flaps up out of the well, drops a child's sandal on the engawa, and flaps back down. Pim: "That one's not mine." 3:00 Pim: "Fine. Drink first." At the hearth the warmth bar appears, the only bar in the game. Light the lantern. Take the furoshiki. Lift the lid. "Go down?" 3:30 The descent: rope, six seconds, warm to cold, kettle into drips. 4:00 Ring one, lit: roots overhead, a comb, a sake cup, a letter; the umbrella watching from a ledge. Two slots. Warmth ticking. 6:00 Ring two, dim: you see edges only; the bell again, further; a glint of gold on the far side; a huge bronze shape you cannot lift. Warmth at a third. The bet: the glint one chamber on, or the rope. 7:30 Numb (most first-timers push; the glint is placed so they do). Soft fade. 8:00 Engawa. Blanket. Kettle. Pim: "I carried you up. Don't ask how." Keep one: the comb or the cup. Pim stores the choice. 9:00 Sudo arrives at a run: "Lost and found? The pass gets everything. Give me what's got a name on it, I'll bring the thanks back. Quick, the tea's going cold." The Dawn Run: forty seconds to wrap, label and bag. He runs. 10:00 Calm again: kettle, Pim, the cup on the shelf, the sandal placed by the well. Then the second descent, and this time you go down for the thing you saw.

## The loop toy

Rooms: 3. The tea room (hearth, kettle, shelf) and the garden with the well, both from the existing scene; one grey-box Hollow of 6 chambers in two rings (ring one lit, grade 1; ring two dim, grade 2, reachable only with the oil andon) plus one sealed grade-3 doorway you can see but not enter. Items: 12 lost things: 8 small (comb, cup, letter, sandal, tea tin, bicycle bell, key, doll), 2 large (torii beam, stone lantern; need the basket), 2 awake (the umbrella that flaps off, the sandal pair that runs; grey cubes with a flee behaviour). Characters: 2. Pim (reusing the Remember toy's memory flags and reply picker) and Sudo (reusing the Help toy's timed fetch as the Dawn Run). Upgrades: 3, one per dial: sencha (warmth 4 to 6 min), oil andon (grade 2), bamboo basket (2 to 4 slots), bought with mon from letters. Systems in the toy: the three dials, the numb fail state with keep-one, the mountain's breath (one fog gate that alternates between two passages each trip), the shelf, the Dawn Run, letters with one line each (12 lines), and the KEEP sink (place a thing in the garden and it stays). Claude build time: 7 build days (day 1 well, descent, warmth and lantern grades; 2 pickup, slots, awake things; 3 fail state, keep-one, Pim; 4 Dawn Run, letters, mon, shop; 5 KEEP sink and the breath gate; 6 the 12 items and balance; 7 playtest harness and fixes), spread across about two calendar weeks with the designer playing each evening. The 20-minute question: does the third descent happen without anyone suggesting it, and can the player say out loud what they are going down for?

## Hypothesis line

I believe drink, descend, gather in the cold, climb, hand it over before his tea goes cold, with grey boxes, holds a confidant 20 minutes because every trip ends in a visible bet (the bell is always one chamber past the warmth) and every return turns the haul into a sentence from a stranger and a piece of the garden; dead if not by 2026-11-10.

## Shared with Dave, and yours

Structurally shared, on purpose: a home/out split with a deliberate threshold; three dials each on its own upgrade track plus a "what you can take" tool; a fail state that keeps one item; two sinks so over-collecting never wastes; a non-money reputation that grows the home; a one-hole conceit that swallows infinite content; systems delivered by named characters at scheduled moments; a fast manual home burst against a slow out. What is yours: the physics is warmth against cold, not air against water, and you take the home's heat down with you, so the two halves are literally one substance; the haul is lost things with owners, so every item is a sentence about a person rather than a food; reach is light, so the dial is visible in the world instead of a number; the out is vertical and dark and you navigate by ear; the home converts by giving back and by keeping (letters from strangers; a garden built from what was lost), not by cooking and selling; the calm-breaker is a sound and a guest rather than a day/night timer; the characters remember what you said, so progress is social; the creatures are objects that woke up; and the thing that carries you home is a mystery with a liar sitting on top of it.

## Three ways it fails

- The lost-things economy reads as fetch quests rather than treasure: if items lack grade, surprise and a voice, returning them is a chore. Test in the loop toy: do players read the twelve letters without being asked; if they skip them, the economy is dead and needs Enoki's appraisal and awake things pulled earlier, not more items.
- The dark is dark: a visibility-as-reach dial can turn exploring into squinting, and grey boxes in grade-2 dim are unreadable. Rule: darkness is edges-only, never black, and the bet must be visible from the previous chamber. If the toy's dim ring tests as frustrating rather than tempting, light becomes how deep the rope reaches and the lantern goes cosmetic.
- The home starves (Moonlighter's shop): if the Dawn Run is the only manual thing to do upstairs, the surface is a menu between trips and the Goal dies there. Rule: every return must have one hands-on beat (placing, wrapping, a reply to Pim) and one scheduled guest beat; if toy players stand at the well-head between trips with nothing to do, that is the first thing to fix, before any new ring.

## The judges

**Designer, 8/10.** This is the one where the designer's existing work stops being a test and becomes the game: the kit is the surface and its hidden pieces (pond, bridge, stone garden, second room) are the reward, so the home grows with zero new art and the garden is a scoreboard you walk through. It has the most alive nouns in the batch (tsukumogami that flap off like bats, lost PLACES as rings, a tengu who lies about carrying you up, a pilgrim on her 33rd circuit, a donkey named Minister), the funniest lines, and an engineered first ten minutes (the umbrella drops a sandal; the glint is placed so first-timers push and go numb). Honest cost: it is the most literal Dave transcription (oxygen→warmth, depth→lantern grade, cargo→carry, harpoon→Hand), warmth is a bar with tea tiers (a timer in a kimono), the lantern grades are key-doors rather than a dial, the Dawn Run is a thin fast beat, and the seven-day toy is really a slice.

*Best idea in it:* The garden comes up out of the well: the kit's own pieces are lost things the player carries up and places, so home growth costs no art and accomplishment is a garden you can walk through (with tsukumogami as the creature class a close second).

*The one change:* Make light continuous and physical instead of a bought grade: the lantern's radius is what you see, deeper rings are simply darker and colder so a weak lantern makes you slow and afraid rather than locked out; reach is then earned by nerve and warmth, not by a key-door purchase, and the slice drops from three dials to two plus the Hand.

**Producer, 8/10.** The most complete Dave transplant with the cheapest home: the 27-piece kit is reused untouched, and the garden's whole growth arc is hidden kit pieces that come up out of the well and get placed, so two years of home progression costs no art; rings-as-lost-places (a drowned village, a lost August, an Edo inn) gives biome variety inside one hole, which is exactly the samey risk the other hole concepts carry. The loop is whole: three dials plus a Hand tool, a keep-one bet enforced by a level rule (the glint is always in the last fifth of warmth), RETURN and KEEP as two real sinks, and a guestbook progression that answers Hwang's regret by making every customer a name. Producer's objections: the toy is 7 days with three upgrade tracks, a shop, mon, letters, awake creatures and a breath gate, nearly double the 2-4 day bar and against Dave's own one-zone-one-clock-one-track slice; and warmth carries three sub-tracks while light grades double as a key system, so the dials are baroque for a first test. Risk list is honest and each risk names a fallback, and the hypothesis can fail on a watchable event (the unprompted third descent).

*Best idea in it:* The KEEP sink: the pond, bridge, stone garden, bamboo and second sakura are not placed by a level designer, they are lost things the player brings up and sets down, so the home grows from the kit you already own; paired with warmth as the hearth literally carried down, so the two halves are one substance.

*The one change:* Cut the toy to the slice bar: one upgrade track (tea tiers), light and carry fixed, no mon, shop, awake things or breath gate; keep warmth, the keep-one bet, the Dawn Run and KEEP, and ship it in four Claude days. Everything cut returns as a scheduled character request only if the third descent happens unprompted.
