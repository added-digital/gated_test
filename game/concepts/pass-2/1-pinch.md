# PINCH

*Night constraint: lucidity.* In Yawn, a snowed-in village in the polar night where everyone sleeps in one Long House and dreams one dream (the village's own reflection under the lake ice), you are the only one who walks the dream on purpose: you pinch yourself just awake enough to grab crisp ideas out of a soft world before the rafters close in, and every idea you carry back up is standing on the street by morning as a building with a lit window, pushing the dark back a tile and the sun a notch closer, until winter ends and the sleeper in the corner bunk nobody can place wakes to find her village already built.

**The player pinches themself to grab an idea before the dream dissolves.**

## The setting, and why

Yawn: eleven people, one Long House, one frozen lake, somewhere in a north with a polar night. No country is named; it is the designer's own north, so every small thing (the stove's tick, frost on the inside of the glass, the exact blue of a two-hour day) comes from memory, not research. The setting is chosen because the concept needs five things and the polar night gives all five for free. (1) A long night and a short day: the dream is long and slow, the day is a two-hour blue twilight, so the pace split is literal, not a convention. (2) Growth that is visible in the dark: a village in the polar night is exactly as big as its lit windows, so every building you add is a new pool of light and the visible map grows outward from the Long House. (3) A reason the dream is shared: in the dark months everyone sleeps in the Long House for warmth, in rows, under one roof, and the custom says 'we sleep together so we dream together'. (4) A mirror: the village stands on the shore of a frozen lake and the dream is its reflection in the ice; you fall through the floor of the Long House into it. (5) A progress bar on the horizon: the sun is coming back, the village believes it must be dreamed back, and the glow on the horizon is Celeste's mountain. The sign on the ice-road reads 'YAWN, pop. 11 (nine asleep)'.

## The village by day

Day is the blue hour: two hours of twilight, about four real minutes, calm, snow underfoot, the stove ticking, nine people in various states of upright. You wake in the Long House with whatever you carried out of the dream still under your hat. The one burst at the opposite pace is breakfast: at the long table nine villagers sit with their eyelids drooping, and you have ninety seconds to put each idea in front of the right face before that face nods off; the right face says 'I dreamed that' and claims it, the wrong face says 'that's not mine' and goes back to sleep, Jorun heckles, Tove tops up the stove, and when the last head drops the telling is done. Then the calm comes back: you walk the one street, trade spare ideas to Pim the peddler for coin, buy a taller hat from Bo, listen to Kettil if you brought him something odd, and watch the scaffold go up where this morning's idea is becoming a building. Everything by day is manual and short, people interrupt you, and nobody fixes anything while you are away. When the blue goes you lie down in your bunk between the snorers, or you fall asleep standing wherever you are and start the night with the rafters already close. The next morning the scaffold is gone, a window is lit, the dark at the end of the street has pulled back one tile, and someone is standing in front of the new thing with their arms folded.

## The dream by night

You fall through the floor into the reflection: Yawn upside down under the ice, every building hanging beneath its daytime self, the rooms shuffled into a new order each night and joined by halls that were not there yesterday. The verb is PINCH. Asleep (the default) the world is soft and out of focus, currents carry you, you drift through walls and across gaps and cannot fall, and your hand passes through anything you reach for. Pinched (hold the button) you flinch, the world snaps sharp, the soft things vanish, the crisp things are solid, gravity is real, and you can jump, land, grab, set down, push and force: it is the only way to take an idea, and it is also the way you wake up. Each room is one screen-sized problem: an idea gleams crisp across the room, the current goes one way, the solid ledges go the other, and you solve it by choosing the moment to pinch and the moment to let go; fall, and the screen blinks shut like an eyelid and opens again at the room's door one second later, one notch more awake. Deeper rooms are older (the Village As It Was, then the Lake Before) and thinner, and somewhere past the far end of each trip is your own bed in the wrong place (on a roof, in the lake, on the ceiling): lie in it and you wake gently with everything. What shifts each night is the order of the rooms, which ideas lie in them, where the bed hides, and whichever new room or creature a villager's request has just added; what never shifts is the verb.

## What an idea is

An idea is the only thing in the dream with hard edges. The dream is drawn soft, through a blur-and-wobble shader; ideas are drawn crisp, with a shadow, on a layer the shader skips, so when you are deep asleep they are the only sharp things on screen and you can spot one from across a room. Each has a name that does not resolve: Sixteen Stairs, A Door That Is Also a Boat, The Smell of Bread, A Window Facing Yesterday, Bo's Big Needle, A Spoon. Each has a whose: the villager whose dream-room it lay in. FOUND: lying in a room, usually on the far side of the problem, sometimes only reachable by trusting a current that looks like it goes nowhere. TAKEN: only while pinched; asleep, your hand goes straight through it. CARRIED: under your hat, stacked, teetering, visible; the hat's height is the carry readout, every idea under it makes you a little more awake, and an idea in your hand (hat full) makes you a lot more awake; you can set one down while pinched and stand on it as a step, which is building, which costs a pulse, and picking it back up costs another. LOST: wake suddenly and all but one fade. BECOMES, BY DAY: told at breakfast, it is a thing on the street by the next morning: a building if its whose claims it, a small thing (a bench, a sign, a cat-door, a lantern on a pole) if nobody does. Or it is sold to Pim. An idea is never nothing.

## How the village grows

The rule: one idea carried up whole is one thing on the map by the next morning. If the villager it belongs to claims it at breakfast, it is a building: it rises on the one street during the day (scaffold, lantern, hammering), stands by morning with a lit window, the dark mask at the end of the street recedes one tile, and that villager moves out of the Long House into it and wakes. If nobody claims it, it is a small thing: it fills a tile that already exists with detail (a bench, a sign, a drying rack, a cat-door), and some small things change what people say. The map is a side-view street on a hillside above the ice: it grows up the slope and out along the shore, and you can only see what is lit, so growth is literally more street. The dream grows with it, because the dream is the street's reflection: every new building hangs a new room under the ice the next night, and that room holds new ideas, so growth feeds the thing that feeds growth. What the player sees each morning: they step out of the Long House into the blue, the scaffold is coming down, a window is lit that was not lit yesterday, the dark is one tile further away, and someone is standing in front of the new thing saying 'I dreamed that'.

## The night constraint

LUCIDITY, built as one button, a frame, a blur, a hat and a sound. No bar anywhere.

FELT. You hold PINCH to be lucid. The character twists their own arm and flinches; the world snaps into focus; the soft things vanish; gravity returns; the hands work. Release and the arm relaxes, the focus goes, the current takes you. The whole trip is that choice made every few seconds: control, or staying asleep. Control is precise, risky and waking; staying asleep is safe, carried and blind to what you want.

DRAIN. Lucidity rises slowly while pinched, pulses on every deliberate act (grab an idea: a pulse; set one down as a step: a pulse; force a shut door: a big pulse; fall and blink back: a pulse, 'did I just... no, go back to sleep'), and rises a little per idea under the hat, a lot for one in the hand. Lucidity falls slowly while asleep, and falls fast on WONDER: the first time you enter a room, the first time you drift through a wall, riding a creature, standing still for two seconds and watching something strange do its thing, accepting an invitation (the goat offers its back; let it). Going along is not passive: some ideas are only reachable by trusting a current that runs into a wall or off the screen, so trust buys reach as well as time.

READ, WITHOUT A BAR. (1) The frame: the screen's edge is the Long House, rafters, the quilt's hem, the stove's glow, Tove's elbow. Asleep it is a thin dark vignette; as lucidity rises it creeps inward and the dream shrinks; near the end you can see Tove's face at the edge looking down at you. When the frame touches you, you wake. (2) Focus: blur strength is the inverse of lucidity. Asleep, the ideas are the only sharp things, so you can always find them. Nearly awake, everything is sharp, and you cannot tell what is an idea anymore: full lucidity hides the ideas by making the whole dream look real. That is the moment to go home. (3) Sound: the dream is underwater; as lucidity rises the Long House leaks in, snoring, the stove ticking, wind, someone turning over. You hear how awake you are. (4) The dream notices: at high lucidity creatures stop and look at you; at the top they say your name. (5) The hat: taller means more awake, and it wobbles.

ENDS. The trip ends when the frame closes (sudden wake, keep one), when you lie in your dream-bed (gentle wake, keep all), or when you hold PINCH for three seconds (chosen sudden wake, keep one, now). Deeper layers are thinner and drain faster unless you know their stories. Late in the game you learn whose stirring the frame is.

## Three dials

- DEPTH OF SLEEP (time): how wide the frame starts, which is how much lucidity a night can hold in total. Track: BEDDING, four steps, from Tove: straw; feathers; the Big Quilt; the shutters. Bought with coin, requested by Tove the night after you come back cold.
- FAMILIARITY (reach): how fast each layer drains. Tonight's Village drains at one; the Village As It Was at one-and-a-half; the Lake Before at two; and every threshold between layers is a long pinch. Track: BEDTIME STORIES, one per layer, from Kettil and, last, from Someone's Auntie: a story you have heard makes its layer drain at one. Reach is bought with listening, not coin.
- THE HAT (carry): how many ideas sit under it before the next one is in your hand and the frame starts running. Track: HATS, four, from Bo: the cap (two); the bread-hat (three); the stovepipe (four); the hat with the ladder in it (five). Each costs coin plus one specific idea, requested by Bo the morning you come home with your hands full.

## The fail state is a bet

There is no death; there is waking. When the frame closes on you the dream is gone and you are looking at the Long House ceiling. The ideas you had float above the bunk, dimming over six seconds; you grab ONE and the rest fade, and Tove marks the wall. The bet is the whole night: every room past your bed is 'one more', and the bed is behind you. You can also call it yourself: hold PINCH for three seconds anywhere and wake now with one, which is what you do when you have the one you came for and the rafters are close. Lie in the dream-bed instead and you keep everything, but the bed is always in the wrong place and always somewhere past where you are. The tally wall in the Long House reads 'Nights: 14. Woke screaming: 6. Chose a spoon: 1', and the villagers remember what you chose.

## Two sinks

BUILD: tell an idea at breakfast and it stands on the street by morning, a building if claimed, a small thing if not. SELL: Pim the peddler, who arrives on the ice-road the morning after you fix it, buys any idea for coin, duplicates included, and coin buys beds and hats. Nothing you carry up is wasted: if nobody claims it and you do not want another bench, Pim wants it, and he tells you what the coast will make of it. (Waking villagers is not a sink; it is what building does.)

## The second progression

WAKING THE VILLAGE. Eleven people; nine start asleep in the Long House. Every idea has a whose; build it and that someone wakes a notch, claims the building, moves into it and gets a want, and their wants are the requests that deliver every new system and every new dream-room. The horizon brightens one notch per villager awake; at eleven the sun comes up and winter ends. The order you wake them decides what Yawn becomes: wake Bo early and the street is hats and knitting and a loft; wake Jorun's cousin first and it is nets, a smokehouse and the lake rooms opening sooner; wake Kettil and the old village surfaces under the snow humps. It is not money and cannot be bought; you can only carry the right person's idea up and put it in front of the right face before it nods off.

## Whose dream

Known from minute one: it is a dream (Tove: 'we sleep together so we dream together; don't touch anything'), and it is the village's: shared, made of everyone's half-thoughts, which is why every idea has a whose and why the person it belongs to recognises it at breakfast. Discovered, down the deep layers: it is mostly one person's. The corner bunk holds Someone's Auntie, the sleeper nobody can place ('whose auntie is she?' 'I thought she was yours'), who has been asleep since before Yawn was Yawn. She is the founder; the dream is her unbuilt village; the ideas are her plans; the deep rooms are her memory of the lake before anyone lived on it; and the frame closing is her stirring: when you act like you own her dream, she nearly wakes. By day she twitches when you describe the night, which is the first clue. The reveal is about whose and what-of, never that, and it inverts Link's Awakening: when the eleventh villager wakes and the sun comes up, she wakes too, and the village does not vanish, because you built it. She walks out into a street she dreamed and finds it standing, slightly wrong, and says so.

## What breaks the calm

Three things, on a schedule. Every night: the frame. The dream is calm until you pinch, you must pinch, the rafters creep in, and your bed is behind you. Every day: a named want standing in the snow when you step out: Jorun's ice-road has cracked and the nets are stuck; the goat is on the roof; a window went dark because the smith's family left for the coast; Bo wants a reason to knit; Kettil wants someone to listen. Each needs something from under the ice, and each one you answer is a new system, room or creature arriving. Underneath, the dark: it does not tick and there is no deadline, but the sleepers are visibly slumping, the street is short, and the sun is not back. The calm is put back each morning when the thing stands and the person who dreamed it is upright in front of it.

## Where accomplishment lands

On the morning walk. You step out of the Long House into the blue hour with yesterday's idea no longer under your hat but standing on the street: scaffold coming down, a window lit that was dark, the end of the street one tile further off, and the person whose idea it was in front of it with their arms folded saying 'I dreamed that'. Secondary landing: lying down in the dream-bed with a full hat and the frame a hand's width away, the exhale as the rafters pull back. Long-run landing: the horizon. Every villager you wake is a notch of sun, and the day the sun crests the hill is the end of winter, with the whole lit street behind you.

## The one-map conceit

THE DREAM IS THE VILLAGE'S REFLECTION IN THE ICE. One map: the street, and the street mirrored below the lake line. Every building type has a hand-authored dream-room (one to three screens, one problem each) that hangs beneath it in the dream; every villager has a dream-self, a creature that is a current with manners (the goat carries, the dog leads to hidden ideas, the crows lift, the smith's dream-self is a door that argues); every idea is a crisp object in some room's pool. Each night a builder script assembles the dream from the village's current state: shuffle the rooms, join them with halls, place the bed past the far end, deal the ideas, go. Deeper layers are the same trick on an authored list of buildings that are gone (Layer 2, the Village As It Was: the mill, the sauna, the chapel, visible by day as humps in the snow) and on the lake itself (Layer 3, the Lake Before). To add anything later: add a building and its room; add a villager and their creature; add an idea to a pool; add a hump in the snow. No new system is ever needed, and the village growing is the dream growing, which is where more ideas come from.

## Characters and requests

Introduces: the sleep rules and the tally wall (minute 0); BEDDING, the depth track, the night after you come back cold (night 2); the gentle-wake bed. JORUN, ice-fisher, the only one fully awake, furious about it. Want: the ice-road fixed and the nets out. Introduces: the first request and the BUILD sink (minute 1: 'go dream me a bridge, since you're going anyway'); the claims rule (day 2); the lake rooms (day 7, once she has fish to complain about). BO, who knits, half-asleep, sarcastic, remembers every hat. Want: a reason to knit something ridiculous. Introduces: HATS, the carry track (day 3, the morning you come home with your hands full); small things (day 4: 'a bench. I knit a bench cosy.'). OLD KETTIL, the elder, speaks only at bedtime and only if you bring him something odd. Want: to be listened to. Introduces: BEDTIME STORIES, the reach track, and Layer 2 (day 5). ## Presentation

2D, side-view, pixel art at a low internal resolution (around 480 by 270) in a SubViewport, both halves. The village is one hillside street above a lake line; the dream is the same street mirrored below it, so one tileset, one art pass and one mirror draw the whole world. The dream is drawn through a CanvasItem blur-and-wobble shader whose strength is the inverse of lucidity; ideas live on a layer the shader skips, so 'crisp thing in a soft world' is free; the frame is a full-screen mask shader whose inner radius is lucidity, with the Long House art painted on it. Why not 3D or 2.5D: the pinch toggles which things are solid, which needs silhouettes that read instantly; Celeste's unit of play needs a fixed camera and screen-sized rooms; the mirror conceit is a 2D trick; and Dave, the designer's own reference, is side-view for both halves. Godot 4.6: TileMapLayers for rooms, one CharacterBody2D with two collision masks swapped on pinch, Area2D currents with a vector, rooms as scenes assembled by a night-builder script, dialogue as JSON with memory flags. A Short Hike is look only, and its look would not survive the pinch.

## The loop toy

CONTENTS (grey boxes). Village: one street; 3 buildings standing (Long House, Jorun's hut, stove-shed); 3 buildable (the stairs-bridge, Bo's loft, a lantern-post small thing). Dream: 8 screens (1 tutorial, 6 rooms, 1 locked ladder to Layer 2 that says 'you don't know this story yet'), 4 rooms dealt per night plus a bed room. Ideas: 6 (Sixteen Stairs, A Door That Is Also a Boat, The Smell of Bread, Bo's Big Needle, A Window Facing Yesterday, A Spoon). Characters: 3 with lines and memory (Tove, Jorun, Bo), plus a silent twitching bunk. Creatures: 1 (the goat, a rideable current). Upgrades: 2 (one bed, one hat), each arriving as a request. Lucidity: pinch, frame, blur, hat, sound leak. Fail: wake, pick one, tally wall.
DAY 1, the pinch: CharacterBody2D with asleep and pinched states, two collision masks swapped on hold, currents as Area2D vectors, drift-cannot-fall, coyote time, jump and pinch buffers, blink-retry at the room door, 3 grey rooms with fixed cameras.
DAY 2, lucidity: frame mask shader, blur shader with an unblurred idea layer, drain and heal rules (hold, grab pulse, set-down pulse, fall pulse, force pulse, wonder heal, idle heal), hat stack, dream-bed exit, the wake screen (floating ideas, six-second dim, pick one), 6 crisp ideas.
DAY 3, the village: street scene, blue-hour timer, the breakfast telling (drooping faces, slot ideas, ninety seconds), build-by-morning (scaffold, lit window, dark mask recedes a tile), 3 characters in JSON with memory flags for what you brought and what you chose on waking.
DAY 4, the loop closes: night-builder assembles the dream from the village state (deal 4 of 6 rooms, bed past the end, deal ideas), Tove's bed and Bo's hat as requests, the tally wall, tune the 6 rooms, cut a 20-minute build and a one-page playtest sheet.
THE 20-MINUTE QUESTION: with two ideas under the hat and the rafters a third of the way in, does the player walk past their own bed on purpose, and after waking with one, do they lie down again?

## Hypothesis line

I believe pinch-to-grab under a closing frame, paid off by one building standing on the street per idea each morning, with grey boxes holds a confidant 20 minutes because the frame is a bar the body reads without being told and the lit window is a reward the eye reads without being told, so each half makes the other one matter; dead if not by 2026-11-10.

## Precedents, and the distance from them

Dave the Diver: shares the slow out and the manual burst home, three dials with tracks, the keep-one bet, two sinks, requests as systems, the shuffled one-map out. New: the out is a dream and its constraint is your own awareness; the home grows from the out literally, since the dream is the street's reflection and the street is what you built from the dream. Moonlighter: shares shop-by-day, dungeon-by-night; the brief says its shop half starves (taken as given, not reverified); ours makes the day a ninety-second manual telling plus a visible build, and the night is generated from the day, so neither half can starve the other. Dreamscaper: shares town-by-day, dream-by-night and relationships powering the dream (here, stories open layers); its dream is combat (from memory, unverified); ours has no combat, the verb is pinch. Yume Nikki: shares wandering a dream for strange objects and the wonder of it; it has no home, no growth and no constraint; ours converts every find. Link's Awakening: shares a world that is a dream; its reveal is THAT and it ends the world; ours is a dream from minute one, the reveal is WHOSE and WHAT-OF, and when the dreamer wakes the village stays because you built it. Celeste: the unit of play, the blink retry, the forgiving inputs, the kind tally wall. One more cousin: Giana Sisters: Twisted Dreams, a world-swap platformer with a dream framing (details from memory, unverified) shares 'toggle which things are solid'; new here is that the toggle costs lucidity and one side is non-agentic drift, so the swap is the constraint, not just the puzzle. Unverified: whether a game already ships under the title PINCH.

## Three ways it fails

- The pinch toggle reads as a known swap-platformer gimmick and lucidity reads as a timer in a costume. Fallback: cut the frame and keep focus, sound and the hat as the only reads; give the currents personality (creatures) and hide the best ideas behind trust routes so staying asleep is a choice, not a wait; if still flat, make the swap a dash-length tap instead of a hold, with lucidity pulsing only on acts.
- The day half starves (the Moonlighter failure): one building per morning is not enough to do with your hands. Fallback: make the breakfast telling the whole day, Dave's bar: shorter, louder, more faces, more interruptions; then let the player place the building on the street by hand so growth is their hand; if it still starves, cut the walk and make the day one screen.
- Assembling the dream from the village state produces trips that are unreadable or samey, because six authored rooms do not shuffle into anything. Fallback: fix the room order per layer and shuffle only which ideas lie where and where the bed hides; make one new room per building type the only content pipeline and accept that the first month ships with twelve rooms, like Dave's one zone.

## The judges

**Designer, 8.5/10.** This is the designer's own sentence built out until it stands up: a village by day, a dream entered by lying down, ideas carried up that are buildings by morning, and a map that is literally as big as its lit windows. For a visual person that readout is the strongest in the set: the dark at the end of the street pulling back one tile, a window lit that was not, the villager in front of it with folded arms, the sun one notch nearer. The dream is one you would want back into: Yawn hanging upside down under the ice, soft except for the hard-edged ideas, creatures that are currents with manners, and the rule that WONDER heals lucidity, which is the only concept here that mechanically pays the player for stopping to look. The reveal is the best answer in the pass to the designer's own worry about dream reveals: known from minute one, the question is whose, and when Someone's Auntie wakes the village stays because you built it. The weaknesses are real: lucidity has too many rules for a toy and could read as a timer in a costume; the asleep state is non-agentic drift that may feel like waiting; the pinch toggle is a known swap-platformer trick; the day is short and could starve. But the breakfast telling (nine faces nodding off, ninety seconds, 'that's not mine') is a proper Dave bar, the tally wall ('Chose a spoon: 1') is Celeste's kind reframe with Undertale's memory, and Jorun's 'I've told the fish' is a character you want to meet again. It is the most alive of the five and the one most likely to get 'that's it'.

*Honours the seed:* Yes, fully: day village, night dream entered by sleeping, one idea carried up equals one building on the street by morning, and the street is visibly longer each day. It is a dream from minute one and the reveal is whose (the founder's) and what-of (her unbuilt village), never that.

*Best idea in it:* Lucidity cuts both ways: control (pinch, grab, force) wakes you, wonder (drifting through a wall, watching something strange, accepting the goat's back) puts you back under, and at full lucidity the ideas vanish because everything looks real, so the moment to go home is seen, not read off a bar. Close second: the dream is the street's reflection, so every building you raise hangs a new room under the ice, and growth feeds the thing that feeds growth.

*The one change:* Make the asleep half a verb, not a wait. From the first room the currents should be creatures you can lean within and ride (the goat in the toy, not day 6), so staying asleep is a choice with reach in it rather than a cutscene between pinches; and trim lucidity in the toy to three sources only (hold, grab pulse, wonder heal) so the frame reads as one thing the body learns. Everything else on the drain list can come back as requests once the 20-minute question is answered.

**Producer, 7.4/10.** The richest texture and the best lines (Jorun's three are the funniest in the set) and the most literal answer to the seed: the dream is the street's reflection, so every building raised hangs a new room under the ice the next night and growth generates the thing that feeds growth. The idea object has a full lifecycle (found, taken, carried, lost, becomes), set-down-as-step turns carry into a verb, and the Auntie reveal (the founder's unbuilt village, which stays when she wakes) is a clean inversion of Link's Awakening. Dreamness is the constraint itself, not decoration. Producer's costs: lucidity is seven interacting rules (rises while held, pulses per act, rises per idea carried and more in hand, falls asleep, falls on wonder, layer multipliers) and continuous rise-while-held is a timer by another name, so the 'timer in costume' risk the entry flags is the likely outcome; asleep is near-passive drift, which is a feel risk in grey boxes; the breakfast telling is a matching exercise with a known answer (the whose is the room the idea lay in), so the day's burst is thin and the walk is a one-building-a-day shop with Pim's coin reintroducing a till; the idea-to-building mapping is loose (what does The Smell of Bread become?); and Day 2 (two shaders, drain rules, hat stack, bed exit, wake screen) is overloaded, so the honest estimate is five days. The six-authored-rooms-don't-shuffle risk is real and the fallback (fixed order per layer) is probably the design.

*Honours the seed:* Yes, the most visibly: one idea carried up is one lit window and one tile of dark pushed back by morning, with the dreamer standing in front of it, and the dream is regenerated from the grown street. The night is a dream from minute one and its constraint is your own awareness of being in one; the reveal is whose.

*Best idea in it:* The dream as the village's reflection under the ice, assembled each night from the village's current state, so the street you built is where tomorrow's ideas come from. Close second: full lucidity hides the ideas by making the whole dream look real, so the read inverts at the top and 'everything is sharp' means go home.

*The one change:* Make lucidity discrete and act-driven from day one (it moves only on grab, set-down, force, fall and wonder, never while the button is merely held), so the hold is free, the frame is a counter the player can count on their fingers, and the pinch stops reading as a timer; this is the entry's own third fallback promoted to the design.
