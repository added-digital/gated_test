# Light Sleepers

*Night constraint: the other sleepers.* A 2D side-view game about a lighthouse rock in a cold sea where the villagers' shared dream is the only mine: by night you hush your way through regions that exist only while their dreamer is still asleep, and by day you build the rock bigger out of what you carried back before the baker woke at four.

**The player hushes the sleepers' dream-things to carry a notion home before its dreamer wakes.**

## The setting, and why

The Lull: a lighthouse rock in a cold northern sea, a day's boat from anywhere, in the dark half of the year. Nobody's country, no flags, no runes, no tea house: a wet black rock, a lighthouse on top, three huts on the terraces, a boat shed at the waterline, a fire pit, sea on every side. The boat comes once a week and sometimes leaves someone.

Why this rock and nowhere else: (1) Cold makes breath visible, and the dream verb is a breath, so every hush is a cloud on screen and the verb reads with no UI. (2) Winter nights are long and the days are short, so the dream is the long half and the day is a precious burst, which is the Dave shape. (3) A lighthouse gives the night watch for free: the Keeper is up all night with the lamp and sleeps at dawn, so the deepest region of the dream belongs to the last sleeper and "deeper" means "later". (4) A rock holds maybe twelve houses, so every house is a known person, and where you build decides who is next to whom in the dream. (5) The sea is where nobody dreams, black water, the natural border of a map made of sleepers. The designer can draw a northern winter from the window without making a Swedish game; the Lull is any north.

## The village by day

Dawn, a few hours of grey light, and you wake in the boat shed with whatever you still held. The day has no clock: you walk the rock's terraces, hand each notion to the person who dreamed it (Margit turns the tiny oven over, tells you that you were small in her dream, and by the time you have climbed to Tove's hut there is hammering, and when you come back the oven stands by the bakery with smoke coming out), talk to the three or nine people who live here (they remember last night in detail and they rate your jolts), and look back at the rock from the water-stair to see it one thing fuller than yesterday. It is calm the way a small place in winter is calm: nothing is urgent until the light goes. Then the light goes and the one fast thing in the day happens: the Lamp Round, ninety real seconds while the sun drops, in which you run the whole rock putting out lamps, latching shutters, banking fires and tucking in whoever asked. Every chore finished before dark means that person falls asleep sooner and heavier, which is minutes of their region tonight; every lamp you miss is a light sleeper. It is manual, physical and slightly too much to do, Dave's sushi bar in miniature, the exact opposite pace of the dream. After the round, the fire: you tell any spare notions as stories, people laugh at what they dreamed, and the Hearsay goes out on the next boat. Then your bedroll, and the page turns.

## The dream by night

You cannot dream on your own (the Keeper says that is why they let you stay), so when you lie down you step out of the bedroll onto the Underside: the rock turned over, each house hanging below its daytime spot as a region made of its sleeper's want, lit from inside, with the sleeper themselves enormous and asleep in that region's sky. You hush your way through it: side-view, screen-sized problems, one at a time, each readable before you move, each strict, each restarting in under a second at the screen's edge when you fall or touch something sharp (a "jolt": you twitch in your bedroll and you are back). The verb is a breath: it snuffs candles, pushes feathers and pages and loaves, puts dream-creatures to sleep so they hold still as platforms, kicks you the other way in the air, and, aimed at a stirring sleeper's moths, buys them a few more seconds under, using the same breath you would have used to move. It is risk-paced because you watch the sky as much as the floor: Margit in the sky of the Proving Room is curled tight at first, then rolls over, then her eyes go, and her region drains to morning-grey, lists like a boat and tips you toward whoever is still asleep next door, or into black, and you wake with one thing. The map shrinks all night, the baker's region first, the kid's next, the fisher's at the tide, and at the point when the rock is nearly gone the Keeper finally sleeps and the lighthouse's region appears beneath everything, the deepest, reachable only through whoever is still under. Each night is different because the schedule is: who you tucked, who is worried, who came on the boat, which bridge you built yesterday, and which of a region's two or three hand-placed notion spots is lit tonight; and there is always one notion placed just past where it is sensible to go.

## What an idea is

A Notion. Form: a palm-sized, lit miniature of a thing its dreamer wants and would never say awake: a tiny oven whose door opens, a jetty you could stand on a saucer, a bell that rings when you jump. It hums faintly in the dreamer's voice. Found: at most one per screen-problem, always at the end of it, always placed where the dreamer "can't quite reach it" (on top of a stack of loaves that keep rising; inside the net that only opens when the fish fall asleep). A region is built around its notions: the main-path notion is medium, the second one in a region is hard, and the Keeper's are deep. Carried: in your arms, stacked, visible, no inventory screen, two at the start. A notion in your arms gives light, so a full stack lets you see one screen further into dark rooms, a small reward for carrying. You keep what you carry even if that dreamer wakes, as long as you are still under; if you wake, you keep one. By day a notion becomes one of two things: handed to its dreamer, it is built on the rock before dusk, the actual building, at a marked spot, with hammering, smoke and a line of dialogue; or told at the fire, where it becomes a story and is gone, and the Hearsay goes up.

## How the village grows

The rule: a notion handed to the person who dreamed it is built on the rock before dusk, where you point. One building per notion per day, no queue, no cost; the build is the whole reward. Every building does something in the dream as well as the day, because the dream is the rock's underside: Margit's oven means she need not wake at four, so her region lasts an hour longer; Tove's jetty is a plank by day and a bridge by night between her Shoal and the Proving Room; a new hut is a black hole in the Underside until someone off the boat sleeps in it, and then it is a region with its own hours and its own want. Each morning the player sees: the rock's silhouette from the water-stair, one thing fuller than yesterday; a lit window where there was none; the dreamer standing at their new thing with a line that remembers your night; and that evening, from the bedroll on the Underside, a new or longer-lit region hanging exactly where the new thing stands. The map grows twice for every notion, once in daylight and once under it, and both growths are the same shape.

## The night constraint

THE OTHER SLEEPERS. You can only be on the Underside while somebody else is dreaming it with you, and each region exists only while its sleeper is asleep. The villagers wake one by one through the night on their own hours: Margit the baker at four, Nils the kid at first light, Tove at the tide, Hesper the Keeper at noon because she only lay down at dawn. When the last of them wakes, you do.

HOW IT IS FELT (no clock, no bar). Each region has its sleeper enormous in its sky. Their posture is the hour: curled tight is deep sleep; stretched out is an hour; rolling over is minutes, and when they roll, the furniture of the region rearranges under you (loaves slump, nets slacken); eyes fluttering is seconds. Their breathing is the region's ambient sound, slow and snoring while deep, shallower as they rise, and from the bedroll on the hub you can hear every sleeper at once, so the night's map is a chord: when the whole rock is asleep the breaths fall into one rhythm, and a light sleeper is a wrong note you can hear from across the Underside. At the roll-over, moths come off the sleeper, three or four lights drifting through the region; hushing one buys five seconds of sleep, but hush is also your air-move, so every second you buy is a jump you did not make. In the last fifteen seconds the colour drains to morning-grey, the region lists like a boat toward whichever neighbour is still asleep, a slow wind pushes you that way, and then the region folds like a page: if you were on the sleeping neighbour's side you are tipped into their region; if there is no sleeping neighbour you are tipped into black and wake with one thing.

HOW IT DRAINS. Real time, about a minute to two hours of the night, so a full night is seven or eight minutes and nobody tells you that. Jolts cost a second each and, in a light sleeper's region, make the sleeper stir. Carrying never slows you; wanting the second notion does.

HOW IT IS SOCIAL. The sleepers' hours are decided by day. The Lamp Round sets tonight's bedtimes: each chore finished before dark is minutes for that region. What you build changes the hours for good, and the first time upgrade in the game is delivered as Margit's own request, because the whole point of her oven is that she can sleep past four. A worried villager sleeps light and short and dreams badly: their region is a nightmare (wrong colours, the sleeper frowning in the sky, hazards where platforms were, an early fold), and the cure is always by day and always something you build. Where houses stand decides who neighbours whom in the dream, so which way a folding region tips you is something you arranged with a hammer. And the villagers know you were there: they wake with an accurate memory of you in their dream and tell you about it, which is both the humour and the proof.

THE SLEEP IS THE MAP. The Underside has no geography but theirs. The night's shape is a schedule the player knows by heart by the third night (the baker first, the Keeper last), newcomers add their own hours to it, and going deep means being under when almost nobody else is.

## Three dials

- Time: the Sleepers. How long each villager stays under, read in their body in the sky and heard in their breathing. Extended per person, socially: the Lamp Round for tonight; buildings for good (Margit's oven: sleeps till five; her shutters: till six; Tove's jetty: past the tide; Nils's nightlight: no nightmares); hushing moths for seconds at the cost of a jump.
- Reach: the Neighbours. Which regions touch, and which exist at a given hour. Extended only by building: a jetty or a plank by day is a bridge by night; a stair between terraces links a region above to one below; placing a new hut next to a late sleeper makes a late-night corridor to the Keeper's deep. There are never movement upgrades; reach is the village's layout, which is to say the player's own hammer.
- Carry: the Arms. How many notions you can hold, visible as a stack in your arms, two at the start. Extended by Bo the tailor's satchel (four) and later his barrow (six, which cannot jump as high, the one time carry touches physics, and Bo warns you). A full stack also lights one screen further, so carrying is its own small reward.

## The fail state is a bet

Waking. If the region you are in folds and no neighbour is asleep, or if you pinch yourself (hold hush on yourself, anywhere, any time: a deliberate exit), the Keeper's lamp beam finds you from above and you wake in the boat shed with ONE notion you choose from your arms; the rest slip. The morning note says what you kept and what you lost, with your jolt count, and Tove reads it aloud and rates it. Walk back to the bedroll on the hub before that and you keep everything. Jolts are not failure: one second, screen start, and the morning counts them kindly. The bet always has the same shape: the second notion is past where it is sensible to be, and the sky is telling you so.

## Two sinks

Build and Tell. Build: hand a notion to its dreamer and it is built on the rock before dusk, the first oven, the first jetty, the first hut; one of each kind is ever needed. Tell: at the evening fire, give any spare notion away as a story ("Margit dreamed an oven the size of a church, with a door for each of us"); the notion is gone, the dreamer sleeps deeper tonight because somebody heard them, and the Hearsay goes out on the next boat. So three ovens are never a waste: one is an oven, two are stories, and the stories are what brings the next person. On a night you woke with one thing, the two sinks pull against each other on that one thing.

## The second progression

The Fire: who sits at it. There is no money on the Lull. Every story told is Hearsay, and Hearsay is what the boat brings: enough of it and the next named person arrives with their own hours, want, region and object (Nils, then Bo, then Gunnar). The Fire itself grows in stages with the roster: a pit; a ring of stones with a bench, so two villagers talk to each other at night and their regions begin to share a room; a hall for the midsummer night when the whole rock sleeps in one place and the Underside is one region. When the Fire reaches its third stage the villagers name the rock at it, in a scene, out of what has been told. What the village becomes is who is in it and what it is called, and both come from what you chose to tell rather than build.

## Whose dream

Known from minute one: it is a dream, it is shared, and it is not yours. You are the one person on the Lull who does not dream, so you walk through everyone else's; the Keeper calls you the sleepwalker and says every rock needs one. What it is made of is discovered: each region is one person's want, the one they would never say awake, and the first small reveal is Margit admitting the oven was never about bread. The late reveal is whose the deep is: Hesper the Keeper, up all night with the lamp, who insists she wants nothing, falls asleep at dawn and dreams the whole rock as she has watched it grow from the top. Her region is the village entire, every house, at night, and the last notion in it is a hut with your name on the door. Your own region, a bedroll on nothing all game, fills in the final chapter with the villagers' dreams of you: you appear, small, in each sleeper's sky. The reveal is not that it was a dream; it is that you were dreamed too.

## What breaks the calm

Calm is the whole rock asleep: every region lit, the breathing in one chord from the bedroll. What breaks it is always a sleep problem. Small and constant: a worry (the flour is low, the boat is late, the kid heard the horn) makes someone sleep light, short and badly; their region ends early, turns nightmare, and takes a route with it. Scheduled and bigger: the foghorn starts in week two and Nils, a light sleeper who stirs at every jolt, comes on the boat; Gunnar the snorer arrives and his snoring shakes the regions on either side of his hut; midsummer, when the sun will not set and nobody sleeps deep for a week; the winter storm that puts the lamp out, so the Keeper never sleeps and the deep is gone. Each is answered the same way: find what the troubled sleeper wants on the Underside and build it. The night the breathing falls back into a chord is the calm put back, and you hear it before you see it.

## Where accomplishment lands

In the morning, twice. First in the handover: the villager turning the notion over, telling you what you looked like in their dream, and the building standing on the rock by the time you have walked past, accomplishment with a name attached (Hwang's regret, avoided). Second at the water-stair, where you look at the whole rock and it is fuller than yesterday and you know why every piece is there. The larger landing is the morning after a broken night is mended: everyone slept through, Margit got up at six and says so, the breathing from the bedroll was one rhythm, and the Keeper, who counts, tells you the number of nights the rock has slept whole.

## The one-map conceit

The Underside. The dream is the rock turned over: every house on the Lull by day is a region hanging below the same spot by night, dreamed by whoever sleeps in it, lasting as long as they do, touching only the regions of the houses it touches. That is the whole content model. To add a dream-place: add a house. To add a creature or an object: give a new sleeper one (Margit's rising loaves, Tove's air-fish, Bo's needles, Gunnar's snore-quakes), the Celeste rule of one new object per chapter and re-challenges on the same verb. To add an idea: give a sleeper a new want. To add a chapter: a new sleeper with new hours. Empty huts are black; the sea is black; the lighthouse's root hangs deepest. Nothing is ever added as a system, only as a person with a house, a bedtime and a want.

## Characters and requests

Margit, the baker (loop toy, night one). Wakes at four, hates it, bakes anyway. Wants an oven big enough that she does not have to. Introduces the time dial as a person: her region, the Proving Room, is the first to fold every night, and her oven is the first time upgrade, built from her own request: "Dream me an oven. A big one. I'll know it when you bring it." Second want: shutters.

Tove, the fisher (loop toy, night one). Wakes at the tide. Counts everything, including your jolts, from inside her sleep. Wants a jetty, then a boat, then for you to stop dreaming near her. Introduces reach (the jetty is the first bridge between regions) and the bet (her Shoal folds mid-night, and its second notion is placed exactly past sensible).

Hesper, the Keeper (loop toy as a presence; her region in full after the toy). Up all night with the lamp, sleeps at dawn, wakes at noon, says she wants nothing. Introduces the deep (a region that exists only late, under everything), the rescue (her beam wakes you) and, much later, the reveal. Request: "Lamp's lit. Don't dream it out."

Nils, the kid (first boat after the toy). Sleeps longest and lightest; every jolt in his region makes him stir; afraid of the horn. Introduces nightmares as the standing break and hushing moths as a discipline. Request: "Make the horn stop. Or make me not hear it. Either."

Later: Bo the tailor (the carry track: satchel, barrow; cloth regions with needles) and Gunnar the snorer (adjacency hazards and the first placement puzzle: where do you put a man whose snoring shakes the houses on either side).

## Later, in order

- Nils the kid (first boat): light sleeper whose region stirs at every jolt; nightmares as the standing break; his nightlight is the first build that is a thing, not a house.
- Bo the tailor: the carry track (satchel, then barrow) and cloth regions with needles; the first bench talk between two villagers.
- Placement: the player chooses where new huts stand; neighbours become routes; the first late-night corridor to the Keeper's deep.
- The Fire's stages and Hearsay as the boat's schedule; the naming scene.
- Gunnar the snorer: adjacency hazards and the first layout puzzle (where do you put him).
- Shared rooms: two neighbours who get on dream a room between their regions, holding notions neither could dream alone.
- Midsummer: a week where the sun will not set and everyone is a light sleeper; the hardest chapter on the same verb and objects.
- The storm that puts the lamp out: no deep, the Keeper awake all night, and the night you fix it.
- The Keeper's deep in full, the reveal, your hut with your name on the door; then boats forever.

## Presentation

2D, side-view, pixel art at a small internal resolution (384x216, scaled), one character controller and one camera for both halves. The Lull by day is a cross-section seen from the water: lighthouse on top, huts on the terraces, boat shed at the waterline, so the growing map is the growing silhouette, readable in one look. At sleep the page turns: the skyline becomes the ceiling and the regions hang below the houses, so day and night are literally one drawing and its underside. Side-view everywhere because the dream must be Celeste-shaped (screen-sized platform problems, strict and fair) and a second perspective for the village would double the controller, camera and art for no gain. Not 3D or 2.5D: the fold, the list and the page-turn are Camera2D and shader tricks in 2D and would be a production in 3D; A Short Hike's look is lovely and would cost this designer the year. The sleepers in the sky are big silhouettes with three poses, not animated characters; breath clouds and lit windows do most of the atmosphere. Audio carries the timer, so it gets more of the budget than usual.

## The first ten minutes

0:00 The boat leaves you on the water-stair in half-light. Hesper, from the lamp: "You're the one who doesn't dream. Good. Every rock needs one." 1:00 Margit gives you bread, the four-o'clock complaint and her request; Tove counts your steps up to her hut and tells you the number. 3:00 The light goes: the Lamp Round tutorial, two lamps, one shutter, sixty seconds; most players miss one, and Tove sleeps light tonight because of it. 4:00 The fire, nothing to tell yet; bedroll; the page turns. The Underside: your bedroll on nothing, the Proving Room lit to the left, the Shoal lit to the right, the lighthouse's root black. You go left. 4:30 to 7:00 Three screens of the Proving Room teach hush: snuff a candle to wake a moth that carries you; push a feather to make a step; hush a loaf to stop it rising; the recoil jump across the flour-fog. First jolt, first one-second retry. 7:00 The Oven, on a stack of rising loaves, taken. Margit in the sky rolls over, the loaves slump, moths come off her, the room goes blue. The Shutters notion is visible one screen on. Push or run. 8:30 Either you made the bedroll with the Oven; or the room listed and tipped you into Tove's Shoal with it (she is still under if you tucked her lamp); or it tipped you into black and you kept the one thing you had anyway. 9:00 Morning. Margit turns the tiny oven over: "You were in my dream. You were small, and you took my oven. Give it here." Hammering while you walk up to Tove. 10:00 Tove: "Fourteen jolts. I counted." Behind you, smoke from the new oven; from the water-stair the rock is one thing fuller, and tonight Margit sleeps until five.

## The loop toy

CONTENTS. Village: 5 fixed buildings (boat shed with your bedroll, Margit's bakery, Tove's hut, the lighthouse, the fire pit) plus 2 buildable (oven, jetty) on marked spots. Dream: 11 screens (hub 1; Margit's Proving Room 4; Tove's Shoal 4; the Keeper's Lamp Room 2, which appears late and is only a taste). Dream objects: 5 (candle, moth, feather, rising loaf, net). Notions: 4 kinds in 6 placements (Oven and Shutters in the Proving Room, Jetty and Bell in the Shoal; Shutters and Bell are the past-sensible ones). Characters: 3 (Margit, Tove, Hesper), about 40 lines between them, 6 memory flags (jolt count, what you kept, what you lost, whose dream you were in, what you built, what you told). Upgrades: 3, all social (oven: Margit +1h; jetty: Tove +30min and the bridge; shutters: Margit +1h); carry fixed at 2. The Lamp Round with 3 chores. The fire with Tell. Night length about five real minutes in the toy, no clock shown.

DAY 1 (Claude): the hush platformer in grey boxes. CharacterBody2D, jump with coyote time and input buffering, hush in four directions with recoil and a direction assist, candle/moth/feather, three test screens with Camera2D limits per screen, jolt-and-retry at the screen edge in under a second.
DAY 2: the Underside. The hub; the region container with a sleeper silhouette in three poses driven by a schedule table; breathing audio tempo tied to the pose; moths at the roll-over; the drain to grey; the list and the wind; the fold; tipping into a neighbour or into black; the keep-one screen; eight region screens in grey boxes with loaves and nets.
DAY 3: the day. The side-view rock with five buildings and two build spots; three NPCs with dialogue and memory flags; handover, three-second build, silhouette change; the Lamp Round as a ninety-second burst with three chores that set tonight's schedule table; the fire with Tell and a Hearsay counter.
DAY 4: the glue. Page-turn transition, save, placeholder audio (breath loops are the timer, so they come first), the schedule tuned so Margit folds about halfway through the night, an exported build, and a one-page sheet of what to watch in the test.

THE 20-MINUTE QUESTION: does the sleeper in the sky work as a timer? Concretely: can a player, with no bar, say "she's waking" out loud before the grey comes, and does knowing it make them push for the second notion at least once instead of always running for bed?

## Hypothesis line

I believe sleep, hush through two sleepers' regions that fold as their dreamers wake, wake with one thing or make it home with two, hand it over and watch the rock gain one building, with grey boxes, holds a confidant 20 minutes because the end of every trip is read in a body in the sky rather than a bar, which turns the last minute of each region into a choice the player made, and the building standing on the rock the next morning is proof the choice counted; dead if not by 2026-11-10.

## Precedents, and the distance from them

Shared with Dave the Diver: explore, return, convert, upgrade, interact; three dials each with its own track; the fail state as a bet that keeps one thing; two uses for everything brought back; a manual burst at home at the opposite pace (the Lamp Round is the sushi bar, ninety seconds long); every system arriving as a named person's request; a one-map conceit so content is people and houses, not systems. Shared with Moonlighter: a home half and an out half separated by the sun; the disease it inherits is the starved home, and the answer is that the day is short, physical and social rather than a shop. Shared with Dreamscaper: a town by day, a dream by night, relationships feeding the dream; taken further here, in that relationships do not power the dream, they are its geography and its clock (as I recall, Dreamscaper's dream is a combat roguelike and the town grants abilities and gifts; unverified in detail). Shared with Yume Nikki: a dream walked and collected from, strange rooms as the point; different in that it is never your dream, and the collecting builds a daytime place. Shared with Link's Awakening: a world that is a dream; different in that this is known in minute one, the reveal is whose and what rather than that, and the dream does not end when it is understood, the rock keeps dreaming you. Shared with Recettear: a deadline as the engine; here the deadline is a person's alarm, per region, plural and social. New, as far as I know (unverified; I cannot name a game that does it): the timer as a sleeper's body in the sky; regions that exist only while a named person is asleep, so the map is a bedtime schedule; the time upgrade delivered as caring for someone's sleep; and the home map and the out map being the same drawing and its underside. Whether the title Light Sleepers is already taken is unverified.

## Three ways it fails

- The sky timer is not read: players get folded without having noticed Margit roll over and call it unfair. Fallback, in order: make the roll-over louder (a yawn in her voice through the whole region; the notion in your arms hums 'she's up'); then a small sleeping face at the top of the screen that changes pose with her, a face, never a bar. If that still fails, the constraint simplifies to the Keeper's beam sweeping the Underside and waking you when it finds you, a visible clock that keeps the sleepers as the map.
- The day starves, Moonlighter's disease: handing over notions becomes a chore between nights. Fallback: fatten the Lamp Round (more chores than seconds, chores that interact, like a shutter that will not latch until the lamp is out) and keep every build scene to three seconds and a silhouette change; if the day still reads as a menu, collapse it to the water-stair look, the handover line and the Lamp Round, with all talk moved to the fire.
- Hush is too thin a verb for thirty screens, or the recoil fights the platforming. Fallback: hold the Celeste line, never a new verb, one new object per sleeper, and spend the fix budget on objects (loaves, nets, moths, needles, snore-quakes); if the verb itself fails the 20 minutes, replace it with jump-and-grab and keep the sleepers, because the sleepers are the concept and the verb is the vehicle.

## The judges

**Designer, 8/10.** The most original constraint in the set and the one that fits the designer's Goal texture best. Calm is the whole rock asleep, the breathing from the bedroll in one chord; what breaks it is always a sleep problem (a worry, a snorer, the foghorn, midsummer); what puts it back is something you build for the person who is sleeping badly; and you hear the calm return before you see it. Time, growth and character are one object: Margit's oven is a building on the rock, an hour more of her region in the dream, and her own request. Reach is the village's layout, so where you put a hut with a hammer decides which way a folding region tips you, which is the deepest fusion of 'the village grows from the dream' in the pass. Tove is the best-written character of the five ('You were in my dream again. You were a fish. A bad one.'). The Underside, the rock turned over with giant sleepers in each region's sky, is a strong and strange place to go back into. Against Pinch it loses on the body (hush is a breath and the sky is read with the eyes; nothing changes in the hands), on the verb (thin, admitted), on Dave generosity (no reach items at all, which a Dave lover may miss), and on the risk that players get folded without having noticed Margit roll over and call it unfair. The rock's growth is a fuller silhouette rather than a street pushing back the dark, which is honest but smaller to the eye. Essentially tied with Heavy Sleeper; ranked above it because the concept's engine (people as the map and the clock) is the one nobody else has and the one the designer would talk about afterwards.

*Honours the seed:* Yes: the day is a village (a rock of named houses), the night is their shared dream entered by lying down, a notion handed to its dreamer is a building on the rock before dusk, and every building also changes the dream's hours and routes. Known as a dream from minute one; the reveal is that you were dreamed too.

*Best idea in it:* The time dial is a person: each dream region exists only while its sleeper is asleep, their posture in the sky is the hour, their breathing is the sound, and you extend the night by caring for someone's sleep by day (the Lamp Round for tonight, an oven for good). The map is a bedtime schedule.

*The one change:* Bring placement into the loop toy. The toy fixes the jetty and oven to marked spots, but reach-as-layout (where a hut stands decides which regions touch and which way a fold tips you) is the engine no other concept has, and it is untested in the 20 minutes. Let the jetty go on either side of Tove's hut so the choice of side is the choice of which region it bridges; if that one decision lands, the concept is proven; if it does not, the sky timer alone has to carry it.

**Producer, 7.6/10.** The most original constraint and the best day half. Regions exist only while their dreamer sleeps, the sleeper's posture in the sky is the timer, and the Lamp Round is Hwang's discovery run backwards: the home half gives the out its reason, in minutes, per person.
