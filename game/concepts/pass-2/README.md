## The judges' verdicts

**Designer lens.** Put PINCH in front of the designer, with two pages beside it: Light Sleepers' sleeper-in-the-sky and Heavy Sleeper's weight, shown as the two alternative constraints and as grafts, not as rivals. Pinch is the designer's own sentence from yesterday and today built out until it stands up, in a north that is theirs without a flag on it, with the most visible growth readout of the set (a village in the polar night is exactly as big as its lit windows; one idea carried up is one lit window and the dark one tile further off), a dream worth going back into (the village upside down under the ice, soft except for the hard-edged ideas, and a game that mechanically rewards looking at strange things), and the best handling of the designer's own worry about 'revealed later that it's a dream': it is a dream from minute one, the question is whose, and when the dreamer wakes the village stays because you built it. Its risks are honest (lucidity is overbuilt for a toy; the asleep state could feel like waiting; the pinch toggle is a known trick) and each has a named fallback. Does the best dream concept beat the Yamabiko baseline? Yes, and clearly, on these points: (1) the seed: Pinch is the designer's idea and Yamabiko is a kit's; (2) visible growth: a street that pushes back the dark and a villager who moves out of the Long House, against a garden that gains a bridge and a pond; (3) the home half: a ninety-second breakfast with nine faces nodding off is a real Dave bar with people in it, where the Dawn Run was a matching mini-game, so the Moonlighter starvation the judges flagged yesterday is answered rather than patched; (4) the reveal: Yamabiko has none that matters, Pinch inverts Link's Awakening; (5) characters who remember: the tally wall and Jorun telling the fish you chose a spoon, against Pim's remembered words; (6) setting ownership: the designer can draw the blue of a two-hour day from memory. Where the baseline still wins, and what to carry over: the ember pot is a simpler, more proven bodily constraint than lucidity, so if lucidity fails the toy the fallback should look like the pot (one resource, three verbs); and lost things that wake up and run from you is a wonder generator Pinch's inert crisp ideas lack. Between Light Sleepers and Heavy Sleeper the call is a coin flip: Heavy Sleeper is the most bodily and the most Dave-generous (reach items, a bridge across cloud, the funniest character), Light Sleepers is the most original and the truest to 'calm broken and put back', and the designer should read both lines pages before choosing, because either could replace Pinch's constraint without touching its village, its reflection or its reveal. Thinlight is the most reasoned and the least alive; its ideas (Type plus Twist) and its clock (the village's morning sounds) are the best grafts in the pass and should go into whichever concept wins. The baseline should be retired with thanks and two of its ideas kept.

**Producer lens.** Put THINLIGHT in front of the designer, with two grafts applied before the toy starts: Light Sleepers' Lamp Round in the empty afternoon (the day sets the night's clock) and the walk home defaulting to the Shut layout (promoted from fallback to design, so every screen is two problems and the retrace is never the same screen). It is the concept a solo designer at ten hours a week with Claude can actually build and test: the cheapest tech, the only honest four-day plan, the cleanest one-variable content model, and an idea object (Type x Twist) that is a spreadsheet of a hundred rows each with a map consequence and a joke. Its weaknesses are known and bounded: Blink is a worn verb, the road is fixed, and the Thinning is a timer; the toy's third-night question is designed to expose exactly those, and if the player turns because of the sky or the kettle, the concept has earned its verb. Keep Light Sleepers in the drawer as the riskier, more original second bet: it has the best day half and the best reveal, and if the designer wants wonder over discipline after the Thinlight toy, that is where to go, with its toy cut to one region. PINCH is the one to mine for lines, texture and the reflection conceit; Heavy Sleeper for the two-problems-per-room rule and the body-bet. Does the best dream concept beat the Yamabiko baseline? Yes, and clearly, on five counts: it honours the designer's own seed (a dream at night, a village that visibly grows from what was found there), its idea object is a growth engine rather than an ornament, its day has a decision (which fog plot) rather than a display, it sits further from Dave (a sweep from the far end and a retrace, not an oxygen tank), and it was written with the setting free rather than with a kit and a country baked in. The baseline wins only on buildability and proven-ness (kit plus reusable toys), and on the richness of its lost-things conceit versus a single road; neither is enough against a designer who has told us what they now want. Keep Yamabiko as the fallback if Thinlight's turn is not seen by 2026-11-10, not as the plan. Two cautions: do not merge all four concepts, each graft below must survive the toy on its own; and every distance claim here rests on the entries' own recollections of Outland, Guacamelee, Dreamscaper and Yume Nikki, which I did not re-verify.

## Grafts the judges recommend

- (designer) Thinlight into Pinch: Type plus Twist for ideas. Each idea is a function for the street (what it opens, seats, lights, crosses) plus a wrong material or behaviour that becomes the building's daily gag (Sixteen Stairs that go down into the lake; A Door That Is Also a Boat that drifts off its hinges at the thaw). Replaces name-only ideas with things that do something odd every morning.
- (designer) Thinlight into Pinch: the village is the clock and the clock gains hands as the village grows. Pinch's sound leak (snoring, the stove, wind) should be per villager and per building, so each person you wake adds a morning sound to the frame's leak (Jorun's door, Bo's needles, Pim's sledge) and the player learns to hear how awake they are by who is up.
- (designer) Light Sleepers into Pinch: buildings change the night. Make specific builds widen the frame or slow its drain for a named reason (the stairs mean Jorun stops kicking the door in at dawn; the lantern post means Tove banks the stove once, not twice), so the village growing is also the night lengthening, and Tove's bedding track is not the only time upgrade.
- (designer) Light Sleepers into Pinch: show the sleeper. The frame closing is Someone's Auntie stirring; let her posture in the corner bunk be visible at the frame's edge (curled, stretched, rolling over, eyes) as the far-end warning, so the clock has a body, and give it to the designer as the first thing to try if the frame alone is not read.
- (designer) Heavy Sleeper into Pinch: the way back is a second problem. An idea in the hand already makes you a lot more awake; author each room's exit for the sharper state (the frame tighter, the soft things gone, the trust routes closed) so taking the idea rewrites the room you just solved, Celeste's same-verb re-challenge from one rule.
- (designer) Heavy Sleeper into Pinch: a Keep shelf as the selfish sink. An idea you keep in your own bunk makes that kind of idea cheaper to pinch from then on (a thought you have already had costs less lucidity), so the player has a reason to keep something nobody claimed besides selling it to Pim, and the old rooms get fast as the new ones open.
- (designer) Heavy Sleeper into Pinch: grow down to reach deeper. A building further down the shore (the sauna hump, the mill) is a place you can lie down in, and you fall into the dream under that building, closer to the Village As It Was, so the map growing along the shore is literally reach into the older layers.
- (designer) Heavy Sleeper into Light Sleepers and Pinch: sleepers as physical presences in the dream. A sleeper's breath is a current you can hear and ride (an updraft, a drift), so route planning is listening for who is asleep where; in Pinch the villagers' dream-selves are already creatures, so let the Long House snorers be the currents.
- (designer) Baseline into Pinch: lost things that have woken up. Some ideas are not inert: they flap off, run in pairs, hide when pinched at, and must be cornered with the current rather than grabbed, which gives the asleep state a reason to exist and gives the dream mischief.
- (designer) Baseline into Pinch: the Swallow. At story moments the dream breathes in and takes a building back under the ice (a window goes dark; the room it hung reappears below with the idea in it), so the home is not safe from the out and the calm is broken by the map itself, on a schedule.
- (designer) Light Sleepers into Thinlight and Heavy Sleeper: the Lamp Round as the day's burst. Ninety seconds at dusk of lamps, shutters, fires and tucking in, slightly too much to do, which sets tonight's clock; it gives the thin day half hands and makes the time dial something you did for someone.
- (designer) Pinch into all: the reveal shape. The dream is known from minute one, the question is whose, and when the dreamer wakes the village stays because you built it; she walks out into a street she dreamed and says it is slightly wrong.
- (designer) Thinlight into all: growth where you point it. Each kind of build eats the fog in its own direction (across water, up the slope, along the shore) so the shape of the village is the sum of what the player chose to build, and the wall map with fresh ink is the only progress bar.
- (producer) From Light Sleepers into Thinlight: the Lamp Round. Before bed, 60-90 seconds of tucking light sleepers in (a lamp out, a shutter latched, a fire banked); each chore done delays one villager's morning sound by a stage, so the day's hands set the night's clock and the empty afternoon has a burst at the opposite pace.
- (producer) From Heavy Sleeper into Thinlight: every screen is two problems. Make the walk home default to the Shut layout as design, not fallback, so the retrace is a different screen and the ghost-fade is read on a layout the player has not done forward.
- (producer) From PINCH into Thinlight's Stack: set-down-as-step. A carried Idea can be put down and stood on (and is lost if the light takes that screen), so carry is a tool the player spends as well as a dial, and a full Stack re-challenges screens in two directions.
- (producer) From Light Sleepers into all four: the dreamer wakes with a memory of you in their dream and says so ('You were in my dream. You were a fish. A bad one.'). The recognition line becomes both the proof and the humour, and Hwang's nameless-customer regret is answered by the people whose ideas you carry.
- (producer) From Heavy Sleeper's Brisk into Thinlight's Lampwright and Pell: say the keep-one plan out loud as legitimate ('You weren't coming up. I know that face. Which one.'), so going past the turn for one Idea is a strategy the game names rather than a failure it forgives.
- (producer) From PINCH's tally wall into Thinlight's hat-brim: record what was kept, not only how often the light caught you ('Nights: 14. Caught: 6. Chose a bench: 1'), and let villagers quote it; the stats screen becomes a character memory.
- (producer) From Light Sleepers into Thinlight's scheduled breakers: the nightmare as a person's worry rather than weather. A worried villager dreams badly, their Room turns wrong and ends early, and the cure is always a build by day; make at least one of the storm nights someone's fear, with a building as the answer.
- (producer) From Light Sleepers' time-as-caring into Thinlight's Pillow track: replace one purchased step (the Quilt) with a build for a villager that delays their morning sound for good, so at least one time upgrade is village growth rather than a shop item.
- (producer) From PINCH into Thinlight (promoting its own third fallback): each building adds a screen to its dreamer's Room the same night, so growth is visible in both halves on the morning after, and the dream is generated from the village rather than merely matched to it.
- (producer) From Yamabiko's Dawn Run into every Telling: keep the catcher ignorant of the Idea. The player must choose who would want 'a Lamp that is a Fish' from one line of want (Orrin: floats; Magda: warm), so the ninety-second burst is a read, not a delivery.
