# THINLIGHT

*Night constraint: dawn.* By day you tell a six-house village on a northern lake the dreams you had and watch it build them where the fog was; by night you walk down a lantern road into the village's shared dream, blink your way through screen-sized puzzles to pick up the ideas nobody ever said aloud, and turn for home before the sunrise climbing the road behind you thins the dream to nothing.

**The player blinks each dream screen between its two layouts to reach an idea and walk it back to bed before the light does.**

## The setting, and why

Nattvik: an invented village of six houses on the shore of a lake called the Still, at the bottom of a valley under a ridge called Dawnback, far enough north that the sun gets over the ridge late. Founded, Magda claims, by people who liked to sleep in. The job ad that brings you there reads "Deep sleeper wanted." Why this and not somewhere else: (1) the mechanic is dawn, so the setting has to make dawn a character, and in the north dawn is seasonal drama: Midwinter's near-endless night and Midsummer's missing night are two scheduled novelties the sky gives for free. (2) The night constraint is read by ear, by who is awake, and that needs a place quiet enough that one kettle is audible: two lit windows, a boat engine, later a school bell. (3) A shore, a slope and open water give a side-view village three visible directions to grow: piers out over the Still, terraces up Dawnback, cabins along the shore. (4) It is the designer's own light; they know what three in the morning in June looks like, and the Goal's "detail in small things" comes from knowing a place. (5) It is not Japan, not a tea house, not a Mediterranean harbour. English-language, invented names, no real place, no required culture; the village is six strangers and a lake.

## The village by day

You wake in your cabin with last night's ideas glowing on your head and they are already going transparent: the first ninety seconds of every day are the burst, and you run Nattvik's one street telling each idea to the person who would want it before it sinks back into the dream (Magda catches anything warm, Pell anything you could wear, Orrin anything that floats), and a told idea becomes a Sketch pinned to the wall in your cabin. Then the day goes calm and has no clock at all: you choose a fogged plot for each Sketch, talk to people who remember what you told them yesterday, sit on the pier, drink the tea, hand Notions to people as Details for buildings that already stand, and go to bed whenever you like. The pace is deliberately the opposite of the night's: the night is methodical (read the screen, plan the blinks, retry), the morning is a social scramble with no thinking in it, and the afternoon is nothing, on purpose, because calm is the home state. Who is there: Magda in her kitchen with the first lit window, Pell in the hat shop once it opens, Orrin at the pier in the morning and asleep in his boat by noon, and later the Sleepers you have summoned, each with a spot on the street and a morning sound. What you Sketch by day is raised overnight by the whole village dreaming it, so each morning begins with the walk out of the cabin to see what came up where the fog was, and the wall map with fresh ink on it. The only pressure by day is the fade, and it is ninety seconds long; the rest is the stability between bursts.

## The dream by night

You lie down, watch the ceiling, count sheep that actually walk past the bed, and arrive at the Head of the Downroad: a lantern-lit road that runs away from your bed, downhill, screen after screen into the dark, and you walk down it. The verb is Blink: every screen has two halves, Open and Shut, one button swaps them, the half you are not in is drawn as a faint ghost so you can plan, you can blink mid-air, and a blink into solid is refused with a flicker, never a death. The only deaths are briars and drops; they cost one second and put you back at the edge you came in by, and the inputs are quietly kind (coyote time, jump and blink buffering, a two-pixel nudge) while the routing stays strict: each screen is one readable problem, the right order of jumps and blinks, hard but fair, Celeste's size. Off the road, at milestones, are doors to Rooms, each one villager's piece of the shared dream, each a chain of three to eight screens with one new object (sheep platforms that only move while Shut, doors that only yawn open while Open, low lintels your head-stack will not fit under) and one to three Ideas at the end, visible from the screen edge so you can see exactly what you are risking. Notions, the dream's loose change, lie along the road and regrow. What shifts each night: the road itself is fixed so the way home can be learned by heart; Ideas you took are gone because they are buildings now; Notions regrow; a Room grows one screen every time its dreamer gets a building; the doors you paid the Lampwright for are there; your entry time shifts with your tea; and, later, the weather over the Still decides whether you can see the ghost at all.

## What an idea is

An Idea is a small glowing object that is two things at once: a Type (what it does by day: bridge, lamp, ladder, tower, bench, path) and a Twist (what it is wrongly made of or wrongly does: a Ladder of Rain, a Lamp that is a Fish, a Bridge of Yawns, a Chimney that Knits). Form: a palm-sized glowing thing shaped like its Twist, drawn in the dream's colours. Found: always at the end of a Room's chain of screens, sitting where that dreamer's want is, lit, visible from the screen's edge before you commit; a Room holds one to three, and Ideas are hand-placed, finite, one per future building. Carried: it goes on your head, and the next one stacks on top of it; the Stack is the carry dial and it lights the road a little; Notions (a smell of rain on hot stone, the sound a ladder would make if it were a bell) go in your pockets. By day, an Idea told to the right person within the ninety-second fade becomes a Sketch on your wall; that night everyone dreams it and in the morning it stands on the plot you chose, the Type deciding what it does for the village (what it unfogs, what it adds to the day loop: a crossing, a seat, a new morning sound, a spot for a Sleeper) and the Twist deciding its look and its one small daily gag (the Ladder of Rain rains upward and fills the well). An Idea not told by the fade sinks back to where you found it; nothing is destroyed, only returned. A Notion told becomes a Detail on a building that already stands: the Fishlamp hums, the bridge gets a bell, Magda's kitchen gets a cat.

## How the village grows

The rule: one told Idea becomes one Sketch; one Sketch claims one fogged plot touching the village; the Type decides which fog the new building eats (a Bridge opens the plot across water, a Tower the plot above, a Path the plot beyond, a Lamp the two beside it, a Bench nothing at all but it draws a Sleeper); the Twist decides the look and the morning gag. The village is a side-view cross-section of the shore: fog sits on plots out over the Still, up the slope of Dawnback and along the shore, and because each Type eats fog in its own direction, the village grows where you point it, and what you choose to build is what shape the place takes. What the player sees each morning: the walk out of the cabin, the new thing standing where fog was, doing its gag, the dreamer recognising it ("I never said that out loud"), and on the cabin wall the hand-drawn map with fresh ink and the fog line drawn one plot further back. Growth is the only progress bar: no number, the map, and the Rooms in the dream growing a screen to match.

## The night constraint

THE THINNING (dawn). The night is a fixed length; you enter it as early as your sleep lets you. The Downroad runs away from your bed, downhill, and dawn in Nattvik comes from the bottom of the dream: the sun rises at the far end of the Downroad and climbs toward your bed, and whatever it touches stops existing, so you walk home with the sunrise at your back, away from the light, which is the wrong way round and feels like a dream. How it is felt: all night the sky strip at the top of every screen drifts from deep blue toward grey, the far edge first, so the screen you are standing on tells you where the night is; when the Thinning begins, the Lampwright's lanterns go out one per screen from the bottom up, each with a small tick, a clock you can see and hear down the road; two screens ahead of the light the ghost layout (the faint preview of the half you would blink into) is gone and you do the screen from memory; one screen ahead the Ideas on your head stop glowing, your footsteps start to sound like floorboards, and the village becomes audible behind the dream: Magda's kettle first, then Orrin's engine turning over on the Still, later the school bell. The villagers are light sleepers; the dream is held up by everyone asleep, and as each of them wakes it thins, so the village is literally the clock, and the clock gains hands as the village grows. The jump is never shortened and the blink never slowed: the dream gets harder to read, not harder to do; pressure in the reading, fairness in the inputs. How it drains: the front moves at a fixed pace, slower than a clean walk and faster than a fumbled one, so depth is time: the deeper you are, the more screens stand between you and bed, each a chance to stumble with the hint gone. How it is read without a bar: the sky, the lanterns, the glow on your head, and who you can hear making breakfast; on arrival, the moon over the Head of the Downroad tells you how much night you have. The bet is the turn: you can see the door to a Room one screen on, the far sky is grey, and the kettle has started. Reach upgrades (the Lampwright's doors) make the walk home shorter; nothing in the game ever makes the night longer.

## Three dials

- TIME, the Pillow track: how early you fall asleep. The night is fixed; your share of it is not. At the start you toss (ceiling, sheep) and arrive with about half the night; Magda's Heavy Tea, then Pell's Quilt, then the Still's lullaby (Orrin's engine idling under your window, do not ask) each get you under sooner. Read: the moon's height over the Head of the Downroad when you arrive.
- REACH, the Lampwright's doors: how short the dream is to cross. A door cut at a milestone links it to your wardrobe, so the walk home from there is one screen and everything beyond it is now near. Paid in Notions, or in an Idea you would rather not build. Later: the Sled, which makes road screens (never Room screens) a slide. Read: the doors glow in your bedroom wall at night, and the lantern count between you and the far end.
- CARRY, the Stack: how many Ideas fit on your head. One at the start; Pell's Two-Hat, Basket and Yoke take it to four; pockets for Notions go 5, 8, 12. Read: the Stack itself, which also decides whether you fit under a Room's low lintels or have to blink past them, so carrying more re-challenges screens you already know.

## The fail state is a bet

If the light reaches your screen, you wake. The bedroom ceiling, one line: "What did you hold onto?" and you pick one Idea from the Stack to keep; the rest drift back to where they lay and are there next night; half your Notions fall out of your pockets onto the road where the light caught you. No health, no money, no day lost. The bet is the turn, and it is yours: the next door is in sight, the far sky is grey, the kettle has started, and you decide whether one more screen is worth doing the walk home from memory. Pell stitches a tally on the brim of your hat for every catching and the village treats the count as a professional's scars; the first time it happens Magda says it was bound to, and that she kept your tea warm.

## Two sinks

Ideas have two uses: (1) TELL at breakfast, Idea becomes Sketch becomes building, the map grows; (2) PAY the Lampwright on the road, an Idea you would rather not build buys a door, the dream gets shorter. Notions have two uses: (1) TELL, a Notion becomes a Detail on a building that already stands (the Goal's small things: a hum, a bell, a cat); (2) PAY the Lampwright, six Notions a door-rung. Nothing brought back is wasted: whatever is not told by the fade is still in your pockets at night, and an untold Idea goes back to its shelf in the dream rather than vanishing. Over-collecting on a good night just means a shorter road next week.

## The second progression

Sleepers: the people who sleep in Nattvik. Non-money, cannot be bought. Every Sleeper is a named person with a want, a Room in the dream and a morning sound; you gain one by building the Idea from a Room that has no owner yet, and the next morning Orrin's boat brings someone off the water asking who built their bridge. Thresholds decide what the village becomes: three Sleepers and it is a hamlet with a path; six and it gets a sign, a fourth sound in the clock, and a second branch off the Downroad; ten and it gets a name, which they argue about over breakfast and you settle, and the wall map's title changes. The mix of who you summon shapes the place too: fishers want things that float and pull growth out over the Still; builders pull it up the slope; a schoolteacher brings children, a bell and the Sleepwalkers' Rooms. What Nattvik is at the end is the sum of who you chose to wake up there.

## Whose dream

It is the village's shared dream, and it is known to be a dream from minute one: the ceiling, the sheep, the job ad ("Deep sleeper wanted"), Magda explaining that everyone in Nattvik sleeps like a cat on a fence and you sleep like a dropped sack, which is the job. It is made of what everyone who has ever slept, or will ever sleep, in Nattvik wanted and never said aloud; the villagers are too light to get deep enough to reach it themselves, and as they wake it thins. Discovered on night three: the Rooms belong to people; you meet Magda's dream-self, cocky, who by day remembers nothing but recognises her Idea the moment you tell it. Discovered mid-game: some Rooms belong to people who are not here yet, and building their Idea brings them. Discovered late: the far end of the Downroad, past every Room, is your own Room, and it is empty, because you never wanted anything for yourself; the village's last build is for you, and the Lampwright is sitting on the bed in it saying he kept the light on. No "it was all a dream" reveal: the reveal is about whose it is and what it is made of, and the day after the last build is still a day with a kettle in it.

## What breaks the calm

Every night, the Thinning: the dream is not dangerous, it is ending, and you are far from the bed with something on your head you want to keep. Every morning, the fade: ninety seconds when the Ideas are going transparent and you are hunting the right person in a street where everyone is holding a kettle. Scheduled breakers so the player never has to make their own: the first night the light catches you (night two, by design, the Room is one screen too far); the first Room with no owner; the first low lintel your Stack will not fit under; the storm night when Orrin says the Still is wrong and the Lampwright's doors are shut; Midwinter, the one night long enough to reach the far end.

## Where accomplishment lands

Three landings, in increasing size. The small one, nightly: the turn made in time, your foot on the floorboards of the Head screen as the screen behind you goes white, the Stack still glowing. The middle one, each morning: the walk out to the thing standing where fog was, raining upward or humming or knitting, the dreamer's recognition line, and the ink drying on the wall map. The big one, at thresholds: the sign going up, the second branch of the Downroad opening, the boat bringing someone who asks for you by name. All three are the calm put back: the night was a trip out of it and the morning is the proof you came home with something.

## The one-map conceit

The Downroad is one line, and every piece of content is a distance along it. A Room is a door at a milestone with a chain of screens behind it; a creature is an object that lives in one Room and later turns up on the road; an Idea is a pickup at the end of a chain; a Sleeper is a door; a building is a screen added inside a door. Adding anything is choosing a milestone and adding a door, and no system needs to change, because the Thinning, the Lampwright's doors, the lantern clock and the walk home only ever care about one variable, distance from the bed. A second branch at six Sleepers is the same line twice. Midwinter's far end is a door at a distance no ordinary night reaches.

## Characters and requests

MAGDA, the kitchen, the first lit window, the kettle (first morning sound). Wants you to sleep properly ("you look like a dropped pie") and someone to feed. Introduces Telling (morning one) and the TIME track with the Heavy Tea (night two). Scheduled minute zero. Remembers every Idea you told her and brings it up when you build the wrong thing next to it. THE LAMPWRIGHT, dream only, a tall person with a ladder who keeps the Downroad lit and does not do mornings. Wants Ideas and Notions for lantern oil. Introduces the REACH track and the second sink (night two: "Six Notions a rung. Don't look at me like that, it's a door"), and names the Thinning for you on night one ("Dawn's at the bottom of the hill. Feel it on your neck?"). Scheduled: cameo night one, request night two. PELL, the hatter, sleeps in a hat, shop opens the first morning you come home having had to choose one Idea of two. Wants to make a hat nobody has worn (the Yoke). Introduces the CARRY track (day three), later Low Lintels and the tally. Remembers every Idea you have told anyone and every time the light caught you. ORRIN, ferryman of the Mild, the engine (second morning sound), cocky, hates water. Wants passengers. Introduces SLEEPERS the morning after your first bridge (day five: "You built a bridge. Bridges get people"), weather nights when he reads the Still (day eight on), and the Midwinter crossing. Scheduled: silent at minute zero when he drops you off, talks day two, request day five.

## Later, in order

- The Sleepwalkers: the animals' Rooms (the dog's Room is sticks and the postman; the cat's Room is boxes), one new object per Room.
- Weather over the Still: fog nights hide the ghost all night, clear nights show two screens ahead, storm nights shut the Lampwright's doors.
- The second branch of the Downroad at six Sleepers, with its own Lampwright lantern colour.
- Midwinter (the long night: the Still freezes, you walk across it to the far end) and Midsummer (no night at all: a day of festival, Sleepers' day requests, the only day the Lampwright comes up).
- The Far End: your empty Room, the village's build for you, the day after.
- The wall map as a photo mode and the tally hat as the stats screen.

## Presentation

2D side-view pixel art at a chunky base resolution (480x270), one character controller for both halves (the village is the same controller with Blink disabled), Godot 4.6 TileMapLayers with two layers per dream screen for Open and Shut. Argued: Celeste-grade screen puzzles need gravity for the risk to be readable (a jump arc is a sentence you can read; top-down movement is not), and the A/B ghost layout is trivially legible as a translucent second tile layer. Side-view lets the village grow in three visible directions in one cross-section, like a child's drawing of a shore, and fog on plots is one tile layer. The Thinning is cheap in 2D: a CanvasModulate plus one full-screen shader that desaturates and lifts whites by distance to the front, the sky a gradient texture driven by the night clock, the wall map a sprite with per-building ink layers. 3D is out because one designer at ten hours a week cannot carry a low-poly pipeline and precision platforming at once, and A Short Hike is a look reference only; top-down is out because Blink wants arcs. Pixel art is the cheapest art a solo designer can make or buy, and Claude can script placeholder sprites and tilesets until real ones exist.

## The loop toy

CONTENTS. Village: 1 side-view strip; 4 standing buildings (your cabin with bed and wall map, Magda's kitchen, the pier, the well-stump) and 5 fogged plots (2 over the Still, 2 up the slope, 1 along the shore); 5 grey-box buildings keyed to the 5 Ideas (Ladder of Rain → the Rain-Well; Lamp that is a Fish → the Fishlamp; Bridge of Yawns → the first plot over water; Bench of Moss; Chimney that Knits), each a labelled box with one particle gag. Dream: 13 screens: the Head, 8 Downroad screens, 4 in Magda's Room behind one door at milestone five; 2 objects (gaps, briars) plus Open/Shut layouts on every screen. Ideas: 5 (2 on the road, 3 in Magda's Room). Notions: 8, respawning, on the road. Characters: 3 (Magda, Pell, the Lampwright), 10 lines each, 3 memory flags each (Ideas told, times caught, hat worn). Upgrades: 3, one per dial (Heavy Tea, the Two-Hat, one door at milestone five). Dawn: sky gradient, the front, lantern ticks, ghost-fade, 2 sounds (kettle, engine). Fail: wake and choose one. Telling: a 90-second fade with two catchers. No music, placeholder audio, grey boxes throughout. DAY 1: Godot 4.6 project; side-view controller with jump and Blink as two TileMapLayers per screen toggled by one button, refusal flicker, coyote time, jump and blink buffering, the two-pixel nudge; screen transitions with one-second respawn at the entry edge; 3 test screens; debug overlay. DAY 2: the Downroad (8) and Magda's Room (4) hand-laid; Ideas as head-stack pickups with cap 1; Notions in pockets; the night clock, sky gradient, the Thinning front consuming screens from the far end with lantern ticks and ghost-fade; the wake-and-choose-one screen. DAY 3: the village strip with 4 buildings and 5 fog plots; waking; the 90-second Telling fade with Magda and Pell as catchers and the Sketch on the wall; plot choice; the overnight raise and morning reveal with the fog plot clearing and the wall-map ink; Magda's and Pell's dialogue with memory flags. DAY 4: Heavy Tea (latency), the Two-Hat (cap 2), the Lampwright and one door paid in Notions; the two morning sounds; tuning the front's pace against a clean walk and a fumbled one; a one-page playtest sheet. THE 20-MINUTE QUESTION: on the third night, does the player turn for home because of something they saw or heard (the sky, a lantern tick, the kettle) rather than because the light caught them, and after that morning's building do they go to bed a fourth time without being told to?

## Hypothesis line

I believe walk-down / blink-through / turn-back-before-the-light / tell-it-at-breakfast / wake-to-it-standing-there with grey boxes holds a confidant 20 minutes because the turn is a decision the player can see coming in the sky and hear in the kettle, and the next morning pays it off with a thing standing where fog was; dead if not by 2026-11-10.

## Precedents, and the distance from them

Dave the Diver: shares the whole skeleton (a slow out and a manual burst at home, three dials with their own tracks, a fail state that is a bet with one kept thing, two sinks, a non-money progression deciding what home becomes, every system as a named character's request, scheduled novelty); new here: the clock is a sweep that comes from the far end rather than a tank that empties, the out must be retraced (the walk home is the risk, not the descent), and home grows as a map rather than a bar's rank. Moonlighter: shop by day, dungeon by night, and per the brief its shop half starves; Thinlight's day half is not a shop, has no money, and its burst is social (telling) rather than retail, with the growth landing on the map; the starving risk is named below. Dreamscaper: town by day and dream dungeon by night, with relationships powering the dream; Thinlight's dream is not combat, and relationships do not power it: the dream belongs to the villagers and the villagers are the clock, not the fuel. Yume Nikki: dream wandering and collecting effects that change you; shared: the wrongness of found things and the bedroom as hub; new: a home that grows from the finds, a nightly clock, and screen-sized problems instead of wandering (Yume Nikki's specifics, such as how many effects it has, I have not re-checked: unverified). Link's Awakening: the island is a dream and the reveal ends it; Thinlight is a known dream from minute one and its reveals are about whose and what it is made of, and the day after the end is a day. Recettear: a deadline as the engine; here the deadline is nightly and per trip, dawn rather than debt, and failing it costs a choice, not the game. Prior art for the A/B Blink swap: Outland's polarity swap and Guacamelee's dimension swap, from memory, unverified; the Celeste contribution is the screen as unit, the one-second retry and the kind inputs under a strict puzzle, not the swap. Sleep latency as an upgrade track and a village's morning sounds as the only clock: I cannot name a precedent for either; treat both as unverified novelty rather than confirmed firsts.

## Three ways it fails

- The Thinning reads as Dave's oxygen with paint: a timer in disguise, and the sweep feels like being chased rather than deciding. Fallback: make the front discrete (one lantern out per screen, one tick, one audible villager per stage) so it is a countdown the player can count on their fingers, and keep the last lantern always one screen from the bed so the turn is a choice with a known price, never a surprise.
- Retracing the same screens home is boring, or worse, the ghost-fade makes it unfair. Fallback: the walk home defaults to the Shut layout so known screens read differently on the return; if still dull, cut the Downroad shorter and put the Lampwright's first door earlier so most of a night is Rooms, not road; if unfair, fade the ghost only on road screens and leave Room screens readable.
- The Idea-to-building pipeline is a shop with extra steps and the village never feels like it is growing, so the morning does not pay for the night (Moonlighter's starved half). Fallback: every build must change something functional in the day loop (a crossing, a new spot, a new morning sound, a Sleeper seat) and the wall-map ink must animate each morning; if still flat, make the dream mirror the village harder: each building adds a screen to its dreamer's Room the same night, so growth is visible in both halves.

## The judges

**Designer, 7/10.** The best 'what is an idea' in the pass and the best growth rule: Type plus Twist, so a Ladder of Rain is a ladder for the village and rains upward into the well every morning, and each Type eats fog in its own direction so the village grows where you point it and the wall map gets fresh ink. That is detail in small things, humour and visible growth in one object. Dawn rising from the far end of the dream so you walk home away from the light is a true dream inversion, and the villagers as the clock (kettle, engine, school bell; the clock gains hands as the village grows) is a graft every other concept should take. But the verb is the weakest of the four: Blink is a stock A/B layout swap with no connection to dream, dawn or village, and the Downroad is a corridor with doors rather than a place, which makes it the dream least worth going back into. The constraint is read, not felt: the author says so ('pressure in the reading, fairness in the inputs'), and the walk home retraces the same screens with the hint removed, which is the risk the author names first. It is the most carefully reasoned of the four and the least alive; it reads as a design document where Pinch reads as a place.

*Honours the seed:* Yes: a village by day, a dream entered by sleeping, ideas told at breakfast raised overnight on the plots you chose, the fog line drawn back one plot each morning on a hand-drawn map. A dream from minute one; the reveals are whose rooms, who is not here yet, and that the empty room at the far end is yours.

*Best idea in it:* Type plus Twist: an idea is what it does for the village (bridge, lamp, tower, bench: which fog it eats, in which direction) and what it is wrongly made of (rain, a fish, yawns, knitting: how it looks and its one daily gag). The fogged-plot map that grows where you point it is the same idea seen from above.

*The one change:* Tie Blink to light so the verb belongs to the dream and the clock. The Open and Shut layouts are what is lit and what is dark; you swap a screen by snuffing or lighting its lantern, so the Thinning (lanterns going out from the bottom) rewrites the screens on the walk home instead of only hiding the ghost. The walk home becomes a different puzzle in the same rooms, the Lampwright's lanterns are both timer and tool, and the stock swap gets a reason to exist here.

**Producer, 8/10.** The most producible of the five. Three dials each with a named track (Magda's Pillow line, the Lampwright's doors, Pell's Stack), the fail state is a bet the player can see coming (the turn, read in the sky strip and the kettle), both Ideas and Notions have two uses with no coin anywhere, and the Sleepers thresholds (3, 6, 10, the naming argument) are a real non-money progression that decides what Nattvik is. The one-map conceit is the cleanest in the set: the Downroad is a line and every piece of content is a distance, so the Thinning, the doors and the lantern clock only ever read one variable; a two-year plan is a list of doors at milestones plus a second branch that is the same line twice. The tech is the cheapest (two TileMapLayers per screen toggled by one button, a CanvasModulate plus one shader for the Thinning, fog as a tile layer), the 4-day plan is the only one I believe as written, and the 20-minute question (does the player turn on the third night because of something seen or heard, and go to bed a fourth time unprompted) is specific, observable and can fail. Costs a producer must say out loud: Blink is the most worn verb of the four (Outland/Guacamelee, by the entry's own unverified memory), the road is fixed so Mystery Dungeon's shifting place is traded for a learnable walk home that could bore, the afternoon is 'nothing, on purpose', which is exactly where Moonlighter's half died, and the Thinning is the most naked timer in the set even though sweeping from the far end makes the bet spatial rather than a quantity. Its real claim is that the constraint changes how the verb reads (ghost gone, do it from memory) rather than what the verb does; that is defensible and testable.

*Honours the seed:* Yes. A told Idea becomes a building on a fogged plot the player chose, the Type eats fog in a direction, the wall map gains ink each morning, and the dreamer's Room grows a screen per building, so the map visibly grows from what was found below and the dream grows back. The night is truly a dream (ceiling, sheep, dawn from the bottom of the hill, the village audible through it), known from minute one; the reveals are whose and what it is made of.

*Best idea in it:* The Idea as Type x Twist: Type decides which fog the building eats (bridge over water, tower above, path beyond, lamp beside, bench nothing but draws a Sleeper) and Twist decides its look and one daily gag. A hundred ideas is a grid, every cell has a functional consequence on the map and a joke, and the player chooses the plot, so the village grows where the night pointed. Runner-up: walking home with the sunrise at your back, the wrong way round.

*The one change:* Fill the empty afternoon with a hands-on burst that feeds the night: graft Light Sleepers' Lamp Round (60-90 seconds before bed tucking the light sleepers in; each chore done delays one morning sound by a stage). The village is already the clock in the fiction; make the day set that clock, so the Moonlighter risk is answered by design rather than by a fallback.
