# Heavy Sleeper

*Night constraint: weight.* You are the new Sinker of Brink, a village on a rock shelf above a sea of cloud called the Quilt: by night you sink into the Quilt, where every idea you grab makes you heavier and the heavy ones are the good ones, and by day you tell those ideas to the people who half-dreamt them so the village visibly grows, lot by lot, toward a bridge across the cloud.

**The player paddles down through the Quilt to grab an idea, then paddles up heavier to break the surface of sleep before dawn takes it.**

## The setting, and why

Brink: nine houses on a rock shelf jutting over a valley that is full, to the horizon, of thick white cloud. The villagers call the cloud the Quilt, and "going under the Quilt" is what they call sleeping. Nobody knows what country this is and the concept does not need one. Why this and not water, a mine, or a tea house: (1) The out is always in view from home. Every window in Brink looks down at the Quilt, the way the Blue Hole sits outside Bancho's bar; by day you can see the gold light of dawn still lying on it and the roof of Brisk's house sunk halfway in. (2) Cloud is the body of the assigned constraint. Sinking slowly through air with something heavy in your arms is a dream everyone has had; cloud layers read depth without a bar (light, density, colour, the pitch of the hiss) and buoyancy is the only force. Dave owns underwater; this inverts it. (3) One axis. A shelf village grows in exactly two directions, outward along the rim and down the cliff on stilts and stairs, and the dream is straight down beneath it, so the whole game is one vertical picture: scroll down from your bed and you are in the Quilt. (4) Altitude is calm for free: wind, goats, lichen, thin bright air, a rooster. Calm reads even in grey boxes. (5) It is nowhere, so the designer can give it any texture later (fjäll, Alps, nothing) without being wrong about a real place.

## The village by day

You wake in your hut with last night's ideas glowing above your head like a thought bubble, and they are already dimming: that is the Telling, the manual burst, about two minutes of real time. You run the shelf, light and fast, bursting into doors and saying each idea to its maker ("A hinge! And a... no, wait... a bell!"); the maker who dreamt it builds it, anyone else will "hold that thought" for you until noon, your own shelf keeps it, and an idea you fail to say fades and goes back under where you found it. Then the day is calm: Oda hammers at the new thing on the next lot, Hollin blows a glass float that drops and rolls, Mim complains about the rooster, Brisk sits on her half-sunk porch and tells you how many times you twitched. At noon the Raising: the thing you gave stands on the map, the maker in front of it with a line that proves they remember, and the shelf is one lot longer than yesterday. In the afternoon someone asks for the next thing ("something that weighs about two, don't ask what"), you choose what to fall asleep holding (a float, a net), and you lie down; the floor tilts and you sink. The Telling is the opposite pace of the night on purpose: in the Quilt you are heavy and slow with a tense mind, in the morning you are weightless and quick with nothing to decide but which door first.

## The dream by night

You sink through the floor of your hut into the Fleece, the top stratum of the Quilt, and the moment you are carrying nothing you drift upward, so you paddle down against your own lightness. The verb is paddle: a short directional stroke with a buffered input and a short recharge, three strokes to a breath, plus grab (touch an idea and it ties to you) and let go (drop an idea, pop up a screen's height). Each screen is a Celeste room turned upside down: a readable cloud chamber with snags (dream-thorn, touch it and you jolt), snores (rhythmic updrafts from sleepers), drowse-pockets (dark soft patches that make you sink faster) and, later, blinks and crosswinds, with one idea placed where it is easy to reach light and hard to leave heavy. Grabbing is the twist: the screen you solved going down becomes a different screen the instant you take the thing, because now you sink, so every room is two problems and the second one is the one you planned for. A jolt is a hypnic jerk, not a death: the screen snaps back to the door you came through in one second, you keep what you carry, and Brisk counts the jolts from her porch. Dawn comes as gold light sliding down from the top of the Quilt with the rooster getting closer; the night ends when you break the surface with your ideas, or when dawn or the Hush (the dreamless floor) takes you and you are Woken. Each night the rooms inside a stratum are shuffled and randomly mirrored, which rooms are open depends on who slept well, and the ideas you dropped are lying on the floor of the room where you dropped them.

## What an idea is

An idea is a Notion: a fist-sized, dense, glowing shape with a readable silhouette (a bell, a hinge, a float, a wheel, a knot, a stair) and a weight class of 1, 2 or 3 told by its size and how deep its glow goes; it hums its maker's material faintly, brass for Oda, glass for Hollin, wool for Mim. It is found pinned at the end of a screen-sized problem, and the richer and stranger kinds only exist deeper: weight-1 Notions in the Fleece, weight-3 in the Deep Wool and below; new kinds arrive with new rooms, because every Notion is a villager's half-dream, the thing they almost thought and let go of. Carried, it is tied to you by a visible string: it dangles below you when you rise and trails above you when you sink, your sprite bends with the total, and you can hold at most as many as you have Strings. By day it is the dim thought bubble above your head during the Telling; given to the maker who dreamt it, it is built on the next free lot by noon (weight 1 a lamp, bench or sign; weight 2 an extension or a stair; weight 3 a whole building or a bridge section) and the maker recognises it ("I DREAMT that. I had that and I couldn't hold it, you thief, give it here"); kept on your shelf, it makes that kind of Notion weightless to you from then on, because a thought you have already had weighs nothing.

## How the village grows

The rule: every Notion you give is raised by noon on the next free lot outward from the Square, weight 1 a small thing, weight 2 an extension or a stair down the cliff, weight 3 a building; every third build brings a newcomer who sleeps in it, and every newcomer adds one room to the Quilt at the depth they sleep. So the map grows on one visible side-view shelf, left, right and down the cliff on stilts, and the dream grows underneath it by the same count. Each morning, before the Telling, the first thing on screen is yesterday's thing standing on its lot with its maker beside it and a new chimney smoking if someone moved in; at night the map shows a little zzz over every house that is sleeping well. Growing down matters as much as growing out: when Oda builds the Lower Stair and a Lower House, you can go to bed there and start your sink one stratum deeper, so the village reaching down the cliff is literally your reach into the dream, and the end of the arc is the only thing that can be built across cloud, a bridge, one section per weight-3 Notion, with lights on the far side once it lands.

## The night constraint

Weight is the whole physics and there is no bar. Your body is lighter than the Quilt, so empty-handed you rise at a steady drift and must paddle to go down. Each Notion adds its weight class; your Lift is the total you can rise with (base 2). Below Lift you rise, at Lift you hover, above it you sink at a rate that grows with every point over, so the first heavy grab is a slow settle and the fourth is a plunge. How it is felt: the sprite's posture goes arms-out and hair-up when light, upright with one lantern dangling when even, hunched with strings trailing above you when heavy, head-down and whistling when leaden; up-paddles gain height under load and then give it back, so the hands feel the drag before the eyes see it; the cloud hiss drops in pitch as you go down, your breathing slows, and the screen's light goes from gold to blue to brown wool. Snores are the counter-force: sleepers breathe in rhythm and their out-breath is an updraft that adds lift while you are in it, so the sound of snoring is the sound of a way up, and remembering where the snorers are is the route planning. Letting go is the relief valve: drop a Notion and you pop up one screen's height while it sinks to the room floor and waits there, visible, for you or for another night. Reach is therefore risk in the body: the weight-3 Notion at the bottom of the Drowse glows like it is worth it, you can see your Lift is 2, and the question of whether there is a snore between here and the surface is the game. How it drains: weight never drains, it accumulates with every grab, and the two things that run out are the night (gold light sliding down from above, the rooster getting closer) and the floor (the Hush, where the Quilt goes dark and silent and only sound remains); sink into either and you are Woken. How it is read: drift direction and speed, posture, the strings, the pitch of the hiss, the colour of the layer, the rooster's distance, the snores. The first time you are stuck under a ledge for three seconds, heavy, the only prompt the game ever shows appears: "Let go?"

## Three dials

- NIGHT (time): the night's length before dawn takes you, read as gold light descending and the rooster approaching, no clock. Base about four real minutes. Track: Mim the Weaver's bedroom line, in order: Thick Curtains, Wool Earplugs, the Rooster Treaty (Albin agrees to crow later, for a price in Notions), each adding about a minute.
- LIFT (reach): the total Notion weight you can rise with; everything above it sinks you. Base 2. Track: Hollin the Glassblower's floats, in order: the Glass Float, the Bladder, the Globe Lantern (which also lights the Hush), each +1 Lift; the rule is that whatever you fall asleep holding comes with you, and the item visibly bobs above you on a taut string in the dream.
- STRINGS (carry): how many Notions can be tied to you at once, regardless of weight. Base 3. Track: Oda the Tailor's sewing line, in order: Pockets (4), the Belt of Hooks (5), the Net (7). Strings above Lift is where the bet lives: you can carry seven and only rise with four, so you go down knowing you will choose.

## The fail state is a bet

Woken. If dawn reaches you or you sink into the Hush, the dream does not kill you; Brisk shakes you awake. A three-second picker shows every Notion you were carrying fading, and you keep exactly one of your choosing; the rest go back under to the rooms they came from. Because it is keep-one, going deep for a single weight-3 Notion with no intention of ever rising is a legitimate plan, and the game says so out loud through Brisk ("You weren't coming up. I know that face. Which one."). The real bet on every trip is between rising with everything and the possibility of nothing but the best one, and the player makes it with their body, not a menu: grab and start sinking, or let go and rise.

## Two sinks

Build: say a Notion to the maker who dreamt it and it is raised on the next lot by noon; the map grows; the makers always have a next thing queued, down to lamps, benches and bridge rails, so Build is bottomless the way Bancho always has a next dish. Keep: put a Notion on your own shelf and that kind of Notion weighs nothing to you from then on, so the Fleece becomes fast and the old rooms become the easy way down to the new ones; your hut fills visibly, and the villagers come in to look. The tension is that Keep is selfish and Build is the village, and the loop needs both: keeping bells makes bells light so you can carry them past Lift to the Bellmaker. Over-collecting never wastes: anything not built is kept, anything already kept is built, and a faded Notion simply goes back under.

## The second progression

Sleepers: who sleeps in Brink and how well, shown on the map at night as a zzz over each house. Every newcomer is a named villager with a want who adds one room to the Quilt at the depth of their sleep; a villager whose want is met and who heard a story at the evening Fire sleeps deep, and deep sleepers' rooms open further down. There is no coin in the game at all, so this is the track that decides what Brink becomes: serve Hollin first and the Hum opens, glass folk arrive and Brink becomes a village of floats and lanterns with a glass hall on the cliff; serve Mim first and the wool rooms open and the weavers come. The village is whoever you have helped sleep, and the deepest Notions exist only because the village sleeps deeply enough to dream them.

## Whose dream

It is known to be a dream from minute one: Brisk says "You sink. Everybody sinks. Most people just don't remember it," and you go in through your own bed. What is discovered on the second or third night is whose: you find Hollin asleep in the Hum, snoring updrafts, and realise every room in the Quilt is a villager's, the ideas in it are their half-dreams, and the whole village is down here every night with you as the only one awake inside it. What it is made of is therefore the village's sleep, which is why it deepens as Brink grows and why the maker always recognises the Notion. The late reveal is about whose again: the Hush, the dreamless floor, is not empty; it is the sleep of the woman who dreamt Brink in the first place, and the village is a Notion that was brought up long ago and never finished, so the last requests are to go down through the Hush and bring up the rest of it, which is the bridge. Completing the dream does not end the village; it completes the map.

## What breaks the calm

Every evening, sleep: the floor tilts and you sink whether you like it or not, because everyone sinks and you are the only one who steers. Every morning, the Telling: two minutes of fading thoughts and doors. Every afternoon, a request the village cannot meet with what it has. And once, at the start: Brisk, the old Sinker, has got too heavy to rise ("Old people sink. Ask anyone."), nothing new has come up in years, the lowest houses are already sunk in the Quilt with their windows gone grey, and Oda has been waiting for something worth sewing long enough to be rude about it.

## Where accomplishment lands

Twice, in order. First in the body: the gasp when you break the surface of sleep with the weight-3 Notion you were not sure you could rise with, the strings going slack, your sprite in bed with the thought bubble glowing. Then where the Goal says it should land, when the calm is put back and is bigger than before: noon, the Raising, the thing standing on its lot, the maker beside it with a line that proves they remember, the shelf one lot longer, the Quilt lying quiet below. The kind reframe for the night is Brisk counting your jolts out loud and being proud of the number.

## The one-map conceit

The Stack. The Quilt is a single vertical stack of one-screen-wide rooms, each with a top door and a bottom door, grouped into strata by depth: Fleece, Drowse, Hum, Deep Wool, Hush. Every sleeper in Brink dreams exactly one room at the depth of their sleep. Adding anything later is therefore one operation: write a villager with a want, build their room (three to six screens, one new object, one new Notion kind, one creature if they have one), slot it into a stratum. Rooms shuffle order inside their stratum each night and are randomly mirrored, every room is two problems (down light, up heavy), and which rooms are open depends on who slept well, so a dozen hand-made rooms play like a shifting place. No new systems are ever needed to add a dream-place, a creature or an idea; the Stack takes them.

## Characters and requests

Brisk, the old Sinker and the Waker: cocky, blunt, counts your jolts from her porch; wants to retire and sleep like a normal person, and secretly wants the thing she dropped in the Hush forty years ago. Introduces the loop itself (minute one), the keep-one bet (first time you are Woken), the Keep shelf (cycle 2: "Keep one for yourself. Things you keep get light. Nobody tells you that."), and, last of all, the Hush descent and the bridge. Oda, the Tailor: fast-talking, remembers everything you have ever given her and holds it against you fondly; wants "something worth sewing." She is the first maker and introduces the Telling and the Build sink (cycle 1), then Strings in three requests (Pockets cycle 2, the Belt of Hooks, the Net), and later the Lower Stair. Hollin, the Glassblower: slow, gentle, has only ever made things that hold still and wants to make one that floats. Introduces Lift (the Glass Float, cycle 3; the Bladder; the Globe Lantern) and is the first sleeper you find in the Quilt, which is the shared-dream reveal. Mim, the Weaver: grumpy, wants quiet, is at war with Albin the rooster. Introduces Night (Thick Curtains cycle 5, Earplugs, the Rooster Treaty) and the evening Fire, where told stories put villagers to sleep well and open rooms. After the toy: Pip, a kid who wants stories (seeds which rooms open), the Scribe with the Notebook (slows the Telling fade), and every newcomer the Raising brings, each with one room.

## Lines

- Brisk, shaking you awake: "Up. UP. The rooster's got you. Don't talk, you'll drop it. Pick one. ...A hinge? You sank past the Hum for a HINGE?"
- Brisk, next morning, on her porch: "Four jolts last night. I counted from up here. Three was your record. Say thank you."

## Later, in order

- Mim and the Night track (Curtains, Earplugs, the Rooster Treaty) plus the evening Fire, which makes Sleepers visible as zzz on the map.
- The Lower Stair and Lower House from Oda: the map grows down the cliff and you can start your sink one stratum deeper.
- The Drowse stratum proper with drowse-pockets, and the first newcomer, which proves the newcomer-adds-a-room rule.
- The Hum: Hollin's room with blinks (walls that open on a sleeper's blink), and the shared-dream reveal when you find him snoring.
- Pip and stories at the Fire deciding which rooms are open; the Scribe and the Notebook slowing the Telling fade.
- Creatures: the Moth, which takes a Notion off your string and must be followed; the Big Sleeper, a whale-sized dream of someone's grandmother whose breath is a stratum-long updraft.
- The Deep Wool with crosswinds, weight-3 Notions in numbers, the Net, the Globe Lantern.
- The Hush: dark, sound-only rooms, Brisk's dropped Notion, the founder, the bridge built one section per weight-3 Notion, lights on the far side.
- After the bridge: visitors from the far side arrive with requests, each one a room, forever.

## Presentation

2D side-view, one vertical world, chunky flat-shaded sprites with warm colour and almost no outlines, A Short Hike's warmth without its 3D. Argued: Celeste's unit of play needs a screen-locked 2D room that can be read in one look, and buoyancy only reads cleanly on one axis, so side-view is forced by the verb; the village as a horizontal shelf on top and the Quilt as a stack beneath makes the map and the dream the same picture, so growth downward and reach downward are literally the same pixels; low-poly 3D would blur the sink-and-rise reading and make screen-sized problems hard to frame, and 2.5D buys nothing the verb uses. Camera: screen-locked in the Quilt, free-scrolling on the shelf. Sound carries half the readout (hiss pitch, breathing, snores, rooster), so it is built from day one, not added.

## The loop toy

Contents: village shelf one screen wide with 3 starting buildings (your hut with bed and shelf, Oda's workshop, Brisk's half-sunk house) and 4 empty lots that fill; 8 hand-built dream screens in 2 strata (Fleece 4, Drowse 4), stacked, shuffled and mirrored nightly; 6 placed Notions of 3 kinds (bell, hinge, float) across weights 1, 2 and 3; 3 hazards (snags, snores, drowse-pockets); 3 characters (Brisk, Oda, Hollin) with about 12 lines each and one remembered fact each (last gift, jolt count, which Notion you kept); 2 upgrades delivered as requests (Oda's Pockets, Strings 3 to 4; Hollin's Glass Float, Lift 2 to 3); Night fixed at four minutes; Woken with the keep-one picker; the Telling with a two-minute fade; the Keep shelf; the Raising at noon. Day-by-day in Godot 4.6: Day 1, the float controller (CharacterBody2D with buoyancy against carried weight, paddle with 100 ms input buffer and three-stroke breath, posture states), Notions as Area2D pickups with a weight Resource and string drawing, let-go pop, jolt reset to the entry door in one second, three grey rooms with snags and one snore. Day 2, all 8 rooms as scenes in two stratum arrays with nightly shuffle and mirror, the dawn light sweep and rooster, the Hush floor, Woken and the keep-one picker, surface wake, the 6 Notions placed, hiss pitch and breathing. Day 3, the shelf with walk controller, lots and the Raising, the Telling fade with say-to-maker, hold-that-thought and shelf, three characters with one-line requests and one memory each, the sleep transition. Day 4, the two upgrades as requests, threshold tuning, one save slot, export, and the playtest script. The single question a 20-minute play answers: with no bar on screen, does the player start making weight decisions out loud ("I'll drop the hinge, I need to get up") by the second night?

## Hypothesis line

I believe sink, grab, rise, tell, raise with grey boxes holds a confidant 20 minutes because the moment you touch the big Notion and start to sink is a bodily "oh no" that only you can undo, and the lot filling at noon proves it was worth it; dead if not by 2026-11-10.

## Precedents, and the distance from them

Dave the Diver: shares the two-half loop, the three dials with separate tracks, the rescue-keep-one bet, the home that grows, every system as a named request, the one-map conceit and the opposite-pace manual burst; distance: air not water, ideas not fish, the home grows on the map rather than in a menu, the burst is telling people things rather than serving them, and there is no combat at all. Moonlighter: shares shop by day, dungeon by night; the research says its shop half starves, and the distance is that the day here is people and a map that grows, not a till, and the haul is for them, not for sale. Dreamscaper: shares a town by day and a dream by night with relationships powering the dream (Sleepers); distance: the dream is not a combat roguelike, it is made of the townspeople themselves, and what you bring back visibly grows the town (unverified: I cannot confirm whether Dreamscaper's town changes physically from dream finds; treat the claim that it does not as unconfirmed). Yume Nikki: shares exploring a dream and collecting from it with strangeness increasing with depth; distance: a home that uses what you find, a verb with fail and one-second retry, and no horror. Link's Awakening: shares a place that is a dream; distance: dream-ness is known from minute one, the reveal is whose and what it is made of, and finishing the dream finishes the village rather than erasing it. Celeste: shares the screen-sized problem, kind inputs and the one-second retry; distance: the verb is buoyancy and the room changes character the instant you take the goal, so every room is solved twice. Recettear: the research names a deadline as its engine; here the deadline is nightly dawn, never a debt. Mystery Dungeon as structural source: taken as given from the research, not re-verified. New here, as far as I can tell: weight as the only resource of the out, dropping as the relief valve, kept ideas becoming weightless, and the village growing down the cliff as the reach upgrade.

## Three ways it fails

- Weight reads as mushy rather than tense: floating controls feel slow and annoying in grey boxes. Fallback: make paddle a crisp dash and shorten sink and rise rates; if still mushy, make weight discrete, so each Notion carried removes one stroke from your three-stroke breath instead of changing a continuous drift, which keeps the feel but makes it count on the fingers.
- The rise feels like backtracking: climbing back through the same rooms heavy is a chore. Fallback: when you are at or under Lift, rising is a fast bubble that skips rooms in a cut and only heavy rises are played; if still dull, snores become remembered lifts per stratum and the Lower Stair arrives earlier so the start point drops.
- The village half starves, Moonlighter's failure: the Telling and the Raising are not enough to make the day matter. Fallback: give the day hands, so the player places the raised thing on the lot of their choice and the makers need help (a thirty-second manual sewing or glass-blowing rhythm burst with Oda or Hollin, Dave's bar-service equivalent); if still thin, cut the day to the Telling, the Raising and bed, and let the characters carry it.

## The judges

**Designer, 8/10.** The constraint most felt in the body, by a distance: nothing on screen, just your drift, your posture, the strings trailing above you, the hiss dropping in pitch, and the moment you grab the big Notion and begin to sink, which is an 'oh no' only you can undo. 'Every room is two problems, down light and up heavy' is the sharpest mechanical insight in the pass, Celeste's same-verb re-challenge from a single rule, and snores as audible updrafts means the route home is something you listen for. The village growing down the cliff on stilts is the reach upgrade, which is the cleanest 'home that grows equals reach that grows' in the set, and the bridge across the cloud, one section per heavy idea, is Dave's legendary progress made visible. Brisk is the funniest character of the five ('You sank past the Hum for a HINGE?'; 'Old people sink. Ask anyone.'). Where it falls short for this designer: the ideas are hardware (bell, hinge, float, wheel) and the noon Raising produces a hinged door or a float on a pole, so the day's reward has less wonder than Thinlight's rain-ladder or Pinch's door-that-is-a-boat; the day is thin (a two-minute Telling and a noon cut-scene, fallbacks admitted); and a buoyancy controller in grey boxes is the prototype most likely to feel mushy to someone who already finds grey boxes draining. The Quilt in view from every window and Brisk's half-sunk house are images that will survive grey boxes, though.

*Honours the seed:* Yes: the village is on top, the dream is straight below, you enter it by lying down, every idea you tell is raised on a lot by noon, and the shelf grows outward and down the cliff (which is also your reach). A dream from minute one; the reveal is whose (the founder in the Hush) and completing the dream completes the map rather than ending it.

*Best idea in it:* Grabbing the goal rewrites the room you just solved, because now you sink: every screen is two problems from one mechanic, and the sleeping villagers' snores are the lift you plan around. Close second: things you keep on your own shelf become weightless, so the old rooms get fast as the new ones open and the selfish sink is also the reach upgrade.

*The one change:* Give the Notions a Twist (borrow Thinlight): each is still a weight class and a maker's material, but it is wrongly made or wrongly behaves, so what stands on the lot at noon does something odd every day (a hinge that sighs, a float that rises a little each morning, a stair of bells). The night already has wonder (the whale-sized grandmother whose breath is an updraft); the day needs some, or the Raising is a queue of hardware.

**Producer, 7/10.** The best fusion of the idea object with the constraint: a Notion's weight is both what it is worth by day (1 a lamp, 2 a stair, 3 a building or bridge section) and what it costs in the dream, and Strings above Lift is the bet placed in the body rather than a menu. Every room is two problems (down light, up heavy), which doubles puzzle content per authored screen, and growing down the cliff as the reach upgrade ties the second progression to the map. Brisk saying out loud that going deep for one weight-3 with no plan to rise is legitimate is exactly the kind reframe the Goal wants. Producer's costs: this is the closest Dave transcription of the four (a Blue Hole of cloud outside every window, dawn as the oxygen tank, sink-and-rise with a haul, a four-minute night), so distance from the designer's own reference is the weakest; buoyancy feel is existential and the thing most likely to eat the four days in grey boxes; the heavy rise is a retrace; lots fill automatically from the Square so the day's only hand is the two-minute Telling; Keep erodes the constraint kind by kind; and dreamness is decorative, since this plays as a cave with air physics and snoring villagers, where the seed asked for a dream world. Two timers (dawn and the Hush floor) on top of weight is one more than the concept needs.

*Honours the seed:* Mostly. Lots fill along the shelf and down the cliff per Notion, newcomers add rooms at the depth they sleep, and building down extends reach, so the map grows from the finds. The night is a dream by fiction (bed, the Quilt, villagers snoring in it) but plays as a buoyant cave; dreamness carries no mechanical weight.

*Best idea in it:* Grabbing the goal changes the screen: the room you solved going down light is a different problem going up heavy, so the second problem is the one you planned for, and letting go is a relief valve that pops you up a screen and leaves the Notion waiting on the floor for another night.

*The one change:* Let the player choose the lot (out along the rim or down the cliff) at the Raising instead of filling the next free lot, so the day has a decision with a night consequence: building down is choosing to start tomorrow's sink one stratum deeper.
