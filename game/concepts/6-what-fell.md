# What Fell

*Angle: start from the people.* You keep a tea house on the lip of the hole where everything anyone ever loses ends up, and people keep walking up the path to ask you to go down and get theirs back.

**The player brings up lost things to hand them back to the person waiting upstairs.**

## The surface

The tea house is the home: a low wooden house and a stone garden on the lip of the Drop, where the afternoon is petals, a wind-chime and nothing happening. Guests walk up the path with a want: a thing they lost, a day they have to leave by, and a reason they will only tell you over the second cup (tea is ambient, never a mechanic; nobody orders anything, nothing is cooked). You do three things here, all slow and all by hand: you talk (choose replies; they are remembered and quoted back at you later), you hand things back on the engawa, and you set the unclaimed things on the Shelf by the door, where guests browse them. A guest who got their thing back and who you kept talking to signs the Book and becomes a Regular: they stay, and each one changes the house in their own way, Marrow's shed, a second bench on the engawa, a lantern string, a bath, music in the evenings. The pace is days: morning (the bell, arrivals), day (the trip down), evening (handovers, talk, the Shelf), night (the Book). What grows is the Book and the house around it, and the Shelf is a quiet second story: a thing nobody has claimed yet is a person who has not arrived yet.

## The underground

The Drop is one vertical shaft under the garden: ledges of fallen things sorted by how long ago they were lost, yesterday's keys on Ledge 1, a grandmother's ring on Ledge 5, and far below, things that are not objects. You go down on a rope-line from the post (no free climbing: you slide between anchors and walk the ledges) with a lantern that burns, a rope that only reaches so far, and a satchel that only holds so much; anything bigger than the satchel goes in your hands, which means you cannot carry two and cannot use the hook. The pace is a held breath: dim, dripping, the oil bar ticking, and the next ledge always showing a glint you cannot quite reach. You gather the thing someone upstairs asked for, if you can find it under the heap, hook it across a gap or pry it out of a crate, plus whatever else fits. Every visit the Drop has changed: new things landed overnight (you hear them thud while you are down there), the heap has slid and uncovered or buried things, and once a season the water rises and closes a ledge while opening a side gallery. You never clear the Drop. You leave when the oil says so, or you bet.

## The threshold

The rope post at the back of the stone garden, past the bridge, on the lip. Crossing is deliberate: walk to the post, hold the button on "Go down?", the lantern lights (oil bar appears), the rope clips (depth marks appear), the satchel opens (slots appear), and you step off. Sound crossfades from wind-chime to drip; the camera drops to the Drop preset. Coming back you climb the rope by hand, slow, and the first thing you see topside is the engawa and whoever is waiting on it. There is no fast travel; the climb is the return, and you cannot climb while the oil is at zero.

## Three dials

- Lantern (how long): oil burns at a fixed rate and the trip is over when it is out. Track: Lantern I (4 min) > II (6) > III (9) > IV (12) > V (16); later a lamp that burns slower while you stand still, so looking is free. Visible: the lamp on your belt grows; the kit's lantern models at three sizes.
- Rope (how far): the rope reaches a fixed ledge and no further, and deeper ledges hold older things, so rope length is reach into the past. Track: Rope I (Ledges 1-2) > II (3) > III (4) > IV (5) > Tove's anchors for the Deep (6+). Visible: the coil on the post gets fatter.
- Satchel (how much): slots; small things take 1, medium 2, big things do not fit and ride in your hands, one at a time. Track: 3 slots > 5 > 7 > 9 > a back-frame for one big thing. Visible: patches and straps. Beside the three dials sits the tool track (what you can take): Hook (across gaps), Pry bar (crates), Net (things still falling), Gloves (hot or sharp), each a gift from a named character on a return, never bought.

## The fail state is a bet

When the oil hits zero in the Drop, the dark takes the satchel. The game stops and asks "What do you hold?" and you pick one thing, from the satchel or your hands. You wake at dawn on the engawa; Tove carried you up, and she has a line for it, and she keeps count ("Fourth time. I am not a stair."). The bet is the last fifth of the oil: the glint on the next ledge against everything in the bag. You never die; you only lose what you were not holding, so every trip ends as a decision, and the thing you chose to hold is usually someone's, which is the point.

## Conversion and two sinks

Everything you bring up goes one of two ways. Claimed: you hand it to the person who lost it on the engawa; that converts into the Book (memory flags, friendship, the guest becomes a Regular) and usually a gift, which is how tools and dial parts enter the game (Juno's boathook, Marrow's rope). Shelved: unclaimed things go on the Shelf by the door; a shelved thing either waits (guests browse the Shelf, and "that's my sister's" spawns a new character and request: the content engine) or gets stripped by Marrow into Scrap, the only currency, which pays for Lantern, Rope and Satchel steps. Over-collecting never wastes: junk is Scrap, anything with a story waits for its person, and the Shelf is browsed every evening. You cannot sell anyone's ring; the economy is obligation, not profit.

## The second progression

The Book: the guest book by the door. A guest who got their thing back and who you kept talking to signs it and becomes a Regular; they stay, and each Regular changes the house by who they are, not by count. Marrow signs: the shed becomes a workshop and upgrades exist. Pim signs: a second bench on the engawa and a mailbox, so new arrivals send word ahead. Juno signs: her boat moors in the Drop's flooded gallery, a second way into the shaft. Sorrel signs: evenings have music and the Callers can be heard from the house. Tove signs: the anchors into the Deep. The house is literally the sum of who decided this was their place, and reach is social too: the way past Ledge 5 comes from a person, not a shop.

## What breaks the calm

The gate bell. One ring means a person at the gate with a hole in them: a thing they lost, a leaving-day they cannot move, and a reason they will only say after the second cup. The calm house now has someone in it who is not calm, and a calendar. (The Drop has its own small break: the thud of something new landing above you while you are down there.)

## Where accomplishment lands

On the engawa, at the handover, in three beats: the thing leaves your hands; the guest says a line that proves they remember what you said two days ago; the guest sits down and drinks, and the house goes quiet again with one more person in it. Then, at night, the signature in the Book. The accomplishment is not the find; it is the quiet after, with the person still there.

## The one-map conceit

Everything that is ever lost, anywhere, falls into the Drop, and it lands by age: the more recently lost, the higher the ledge. One shaft, one map. Any object from any place or era is added as a thing on a ledge with an owner who may one day ring the bell, with no new system: a key, a wedding ring, a tooth, a prosthetic hand, a manuscript, a dog's collar. And because "lost" is wider than "dropped", the Deep holds lost sleep, a lost temper, a lost tune, a lost name, a lost Tuesday: same pickup system, stranger nouns, and the wonder gets deeper the further the rope goes. Content is added by dropping things into the hole, forever.

## Characters and requests

Pim (courier, 15, cocky; the Remember toy's own character, carried over): lost an undelivered letter, "not important"; introduces the core loop (go down, find, hand back), reply-memory (what you said comes back at you) and the Book; the first Regular. Marrow (tinker, sixties, dry, hates stories): wants "anything with a spring in it", five of them; introduces the Shelf-to-Scrap sink and the three dial upgrade tracks, and takes over the shed. Juno Halt (ferry pilot, always leaving; the Help toy): lost a brass tide-watch, and her boat leaves from the jetty on the third morning with or without it; introduces leaving-days (the guest calendar, pressure in days not seconds) and the Hook, her boathook, given on return. Tove (the Lamplighter, old, walks the lower ledges with a lamp that does not go out and says she was never there; the Far sound toy): wants nothing, yet; introduces the fail-state (she carries you up and counts), later the anchors into the Deep and the Callers: lost things that chime when their owner is near the house, so you hear a far sound and go looking, three times, each longer.

## Two lines

- Pim: "You said you'd be back before the tea went cold. It's cold. I'm not mad. I'm keeping a list."
- Pim: "Oh. You actually found it. Great. I'd already told everyone you wouldn't, so this is mostly awkward for me."

## Later, in order

- Leaving-days and the Hook (Juno, story day 2): guests have a calendar and some things sit across gaps. First after the slice because it is the pressure the home half needs, in days, not a timer.
- The Callers (Tove, after 3 Regulars): lost things that chime when their owner is near the house; audio-led searches to ledges you cannot yet reach; Rope anchors to Ledges 4-5.
- Shelf recognition (after 5 shelved things): guests browse the Shelf and claim or recognise things, spawning new characters from objects already in the game. This is the content engine switching on.
- The Mend-bench (Marrow, after Lantern II): some fallen things are broken and need a second part, one from the Drop and one from a Regular; small hand-puzzles before a handover.
- House growth beats, scheduled one per signature: second bench, lantern string, bath, and Juno's boat mooring in the flooded gallery as a side entrance to the same shaft (no new map).
- Seasons (chapter 2): autumn water closes Ledges 2-3 and opens a side gallery; winter ice; the Net tool for things still falling. The Drop shifts at macro scale.
- The Deep (chapter 3): below Ledge 6 the lost things stop being objects: a lost tune, a lost Tuesday, a lost name. Same pickup, stranger nouns; Tove's own loss; Lantern V.
- The Old Well (chapter 4): a trapdoor under the tatami into the same shaft from above the heap, a reverse route for players who want the house to be the map. Still one map.

## Presentation

Low-poly 3D, third-person, fixed-angle 3/4 camera (A Short Hike's angle) on a SpringArm3D with no player camera control; one rig with two presets: home (higher, warmer, wider) and Drop (lower, tighter, looking across the shaft so the next ledge's glint is always in frame). Why: the kit is already 3D glTF and walkable in Godot 4.6, so the home half exists; the style contract plus validator is a working pipeline for Claude to make props, which is the real asset cost of "add things to the hole forever"; a vertical shaft of ledges reads from a 3/4 follow camera and makes the depth dial visible; and descent on a rope-line (Path3D between anchors, CharacterBody3D on the ledges) means no climbing animation or physics. Not 2D because the kit would be wasted and depth would become a scroll bar; not first-person because of animation and sightline cost.

## The kit

Reused whole: the tea house, engawa, tatami, shoji, roof, stone garden, pond, bridge, bamboo, sakura, lanterns and props; the home is the thing you already have, and the Japanese flavour stays as setting, not lore. Adapted: the garden's back edge becomes the lip of the Drop with a rope post; the kit's lanterns become the Lantern upgrade models at three sizes; the engawa becomes the handover stage; a shed in the kit style for Marrow. Dropped: nothing. Added through the existing style contract and validator: rope post, three ledge modules, a heap module, a shaft wall, a lantern-glow material, and fallen props (12 to start). Cost: 0 days for the toy (all grey), about 3 Claude days after the toy passes, because the contract and validator already exist and one prop is one prompt plus a validator pass.

## The first ten minutes

0:00 Afternoon, the tea house. You walk the engawa; wind-chime, petals. One line on screen: "Nothing has fallen today." 0:45 The gate bell. Pim comes up the path, breathless, lost a letter, "not important", clearly important. Three replies; Pim notes which. 2:00 Pim: "Everyone says you go down." You walk past the bridge to the rope post, hold "Go down?", the three bars appear, you step off; chime becomes drip. 3:00 Ledge 1: a heap of recent things, keys, a shoe, an umbrella, a cup. You take three (satchel full). A thud above: something new lands. 5:00 Oil at 40%. The letter is visible on Ledge 2 under an umbrella; the rope reaches. You go. Oil 15% when you get there; satchel full, so you drop the shoe for the letter (the first choice). 7:00 You climb with oil at 4%; it is tight, not scripted, and doable. Surface at dusk; Pim is on the engawa. 7:30 The handover. Pim's line quotes your reply from 0:45. Pim sits, drinks; the house goes quiet. 8:30 Unclaimed keys and cup go on the Shelf: "Someone will come for this." 9:00 Night. Pim: "I'm staying until the letter's... anyway, I'm staying." The Book opens with one name. 10:00 Morning. The bell rings again (Marrow). The loop has closed once and the second one is already pulling.

## The loop toy

Rooms: 2 (the existing tea house and garden; the Drop as a grey shaft with 3 ledges and 1 gap). Items: 12 fallen things as labelled grey primitives: 3 requested (Pim's letter on Ledge 2; Juno's watch on Ledge 3 across the gap; Marrow's 5 springs scattered across all ledges), 9 junk, 2 of them big (hands only). Characters: 3 (Pim, Marrow, Juno), each with 1 request, 3 reply choices, 2 memory flags that get quoted back, and a leaving-day for Juno. Upgrades: 3, one per dial (Lantern +50%, Rope +1 ledge, Satchel +2 slots), paid in Scrap at Marrow. Systems: lantern timer, rope-line down and up, satchel plus hands, the fail-state "What do you hold?" with wake-on-engawa, Shelf to Scrap, the bell and day cycle, overnight falls plus heap slide. No art, no music, text boxes. Claude build: 6 days (1: Drop grey-box, rope-line, lantern, satchel; 2: fail-state, Shelf, Scrap; 3: dialogue with flags and Pim; 4: Marrow, upgrades, Juno's clock; 5: the 12 items, overnight falls, day cycle; 6: tuning so Lantern I makes Ledge 2 a real bet). The 20-minute question: does a named person waiting upstairs make the fifth trip down feel like a bet instead of a chore?

## Hypothesis line

I believe go-down-on-three-dials, bring-up, hand-back-to-someone-who-remembers with grey boxes holds a confidant 20 minutes because every trip ends in a bet and every return ends in a face, and the face is the reason for the next trip; dead if not by 2026-11-10.

## Shared with Dave, and yours

Shared on purpose, because it is the proven skeleton: a home and an out split by a deliberate threshold; three dials plus a what-you-can-take tool track, each with its own upgrade; a fail-state that keeps one item; two sinks so over-collecting never wastes; a non-money progression that decides what the home becomes; a one-map conceit; every system arriving as a named character's request; side activities scheduled at story beats. Yours: the haul is other people's belongings, so there is no selling and no profit; you give things back, scrap comes only from junk, and the real reward is a person. The out is sorted by time, not biome: depth is how long ago it was lost, so rope length is reach into the past and the nouns can go abstract at the bottom. The home has no rush minigame; the rush lives in a calendar (leaving-days) and in people, and the manual home activity is conversation and the handover. Progress is social: reach into the Deep comes from Regulars, and the house is built by who stays, not by a rating. The fail-state is voiced ("What do you hold?") and counted by a character. The tone is cocky and short, not warm. And Hwang's regret about nameless customers is solved at the root: there are no customers, only names.

## Three ways it fails

- The faces do not land: if Pim's quoted-back line reads as a template fill instead of a person remembering, the loop is fetch-quests, which is exactly the designer's chore. The toy's question aims at this; if 20 minutes fail here, the concept is dead, not the dials.
- The Drop is a vacuum cleaner: three grey ledges and pickups are thin unless the dials force a decision every trip (which ledge, what to drop, when to climb). Tuning rule for the toy: Lantern I must make Ledge 2 a genuine bet, and the satchel must be full before the requested thing is found.
- 3D eats the schedule: rope descent, ledge collision and a camera in a vertical shaft are where Godot 3D work hides. The guard is the rope-line (Path3D, no climbing physics) and the fixed camera; the kill check is day 2 of the toy build: if the rope-line still feels bad then, that is the finding, and no content is added until it is fixed.

## The judges

**Designer, 5.5/10.** Honest and character-driven, with two engines the others lack (the Shelf, where an unclaimed thing is a person who has not arrived yet and a browsing guest can spawn a character from an item already in the game; leaving-days as calendar pressure in days, not seconds), the batch's best single line ('I'd already told everyone you wouldn't, so this is mostly awkward for me') and Tove counting the times she carried you ('I am not a stair'). But it is the template with the least on top: lantern, rope and satchel as three bars, grey ledges with heaps, a surface with nothing hands-on between trips (the Moonlighter starvation the research warned about), and the thinnest visuals of the six; it is the one the designer would find draining in grey boxes.

*Best idea in it:* The Shelf as a content engine: a thing nobody has claimed yet is a person who has not arrived yet, and guests browsing it spawn new characters from objects already in the game.

*The one change:* Give the surface a hands-on beat in the first slice (the Mend-bench pulled forward, or an evening Shelf-browse where guests physically pick things up) and give the ledges era identity, so the home is not a dialogue menu and the Drop is not a grey tube.

**Producer, 6/10.** The cheapest build (rope-line on Path3D so no climbing physics, kit reused whole, about three asset days) and the best content engine: unclaimed things on the Shelf are browsed by guests and spawn characters from props already built, and 'lost' widens into a lost tune and a lost Tuesday as the rope goes deeper. The home is where Hwang would stop it: there is no manual, time-limited beat by design, pressure lives in a calendar of days, Scrap comes only from junk, so the surface is conversation and a handover, which is Moonlighter's starved shop half, and the risk list names faces, vacuum-cleaner ledges and 3D schedule but not this. Dial tracks are real, tools-as-gifts fits the obligation economy, and the hypothesis is failable.

*Best idea in it:* Shelf recognition as the content engine: a thing nobody has claimed is a person who has not arrived yet, so every prop added to the hole is also a future character and request without a new system.

*The one change:* Add a manual, time-limited home beat to the toy: an evening handover rush when two or three guests with leaving-days are on the engawa at once and the Shelf has to be matched to them before the lanterns burn down, the opposite pace to the Drop.
