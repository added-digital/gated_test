# What the Mountain Keeps

*Angle: start from the feeling.* You are the Finder of Nock, a village built on the lip of a hole that swallows things whenever the mountain breathes in, and every time it does you climb down with a lamp to bring back what it took, because the village is only as calm as the list of what is still missing.

**The player descends the Hollow to put back what the mountain swallowed.**

## The surface

Nock is a tea-terrace village on the lip of the Throat, the shaft that drops into the Hollow. You walk the terraces at a stroll: Marit's bakery, Orre's winch, Hesper's bench shed, Pim trailing you with the Ledger; nothing up here has a clock except cart day. What you do is put things back where they belong (the Putting Back), hand what nobody wants to Hesper's bench to be taken apart, buy lamp oil, and talk to people who remember what you said last time and hold you to it. The village grows by the subtraction of absence: every returned thing lights one lantern at the Throat and restores a visible piece of Nock (the oven lights, the bridge gets its planks back, the bell rings noon again), and at each Trust rank a family moves back into one of the empty houses and brings a new want. The sakura on the upper terrace shed petals all day; when the mountain is about to inhale the petals stop drifting and start streaming toward the Throat, and everyone looks up. That stillness before a Swallow, and the deeper stillness after a Putting Back, are the two calms the game lives between.

## The underground

The Hollow is the inside of the mountain: black, cold, and stacked with everything anyone ever lost, newest nearest the top. Your lamp is your world, a warm circle a few metres wide, and beyond it is the Hush, which is dark that moves. You go slowly because the lamp burns while you walk, drafts bend the flame, and ledges end without warning. You listen: the thing you came for hums (the Far sound toy, kept), louder when you face it and muffled by rock, so every chamber is a short question of how to get around to it, and some answers need the lamp turned down because glowmoss paths only show in the dark. You gather lost things (owned, heavy, specific: a hearthstone, a kettle, a goat that follows you, a letter) and hollow-stuff that no one owns (glowmoss, old iron, hush-wool). Chambers are hand-built but re-stacked every time the mountain breathes, so the way down is never the same twice, newly swallowed things land in fresh places, and anything the dark took from you last trip is down there again, one shelf deeper and a little changed. Deeper shelves hold older, stranger losses: a lighthouse lens, a clock hand the size of a door, a jar with a whale's song in it, and at the bottom the things people lost before anyone remembers.

## The threshold

The Throat: a stone shaft in the village square with Orre's winch over it. To go down you climb into the bucket and ring the bell; Orre lowers you while the daylight shrinks to a coin above, the camera drops lower and closer, the music stops, and you strike the lamp at the bottom. The rope only reaches so far: it ends at a landing on a shelf, you can walk beyond it, but you must walk back to it and ring to be hauled up, so the rope is a leash that the lamp is always measured against. Coming up is the mirror: bell, rope creak, the coin of daylight growing, village sound returning before the picture does. You never cross by accident; every descent is a bucket you chose to climb into and every return is a bell you chose to ring.

## Three dials

- LAMP (how long). The lamp burns oil for a fixed count and when it dies you are in the Hush. Track: tin lamp 75 s, brass lamp 110 s, moss lamp 150 s (burns glowmoss you gathered), Orre's storm lantern 200 s and drafts cannot bend it. Tiers are bought at Hesper's bench with parts; oil is bought per trip with coin.
- ROPE (how far). The winch rope reaches a fixed number of shelves and the landing at its end is the only place you can be hauled from. Track: hemp (shelf 1, the Recent), braided (shelf 2, the Old), chain (shelf 3), the long rope Hesper spins from hush-wool (shelf 4, the Deep). Longer rope means a longer walk back, so every rope upgrade raises the stakes on the lamp.
- PACK (how much). Weight slots; lost things have weight (letter 0, kettle 1, goat 2, bell-clapper 2, hearthstone 3) and so does hollow-stuff. Track: satchel 4, finder's pack 7, frame pack 10, then the sled that drags one Big Thing (bridge planks, the village bell) in a trip of its own. Beside the dials sit the 'what you can take' tools: pry bar, hook, winch-hook, each a request from Hesper.

## The fail state is a bet

The lamp dies, not you. In the dark the Hush closes and you lose the picture: the screen goes black except text, and you choose ONE thing from your pack to keep, by touch ('something cold and round', 'something that is trying to lick you'). Orre finds the rope slack at dawn and hauls you up; you wake at the winch holding that one thing with a chalk mark against your name on her post. The rest is kept by the mountain, not destroyed: it reappears on a deeper shelf next time, slightly changed (the kettle has a dent and a note in it), and Pim writes 'lost again, deeper' in the Ledger. So the end of every trip is a bet made at a quarter lamp: ring the bell from the landing with what you have, or take one more chamber for the thing that is humming and risk choosing in the dark.

## Conversion and two sinks

Everything you haul up goes to one of two places and nothing is wasted. Sink one, the Putting Back: an owned thing goes to its owner and its place; the owner pays coin, Trust rises, and the thing visibly restores part of Nock. Sink two, the Bench: anything at all (unowned things, duplicates, the third kettle, hollow-stuff) is taken apart by Hesper into parts (brass, wick, cord, moss) that buy the dial upgrades, and spare parts sell to Dunn's cart for coin. Coin's job is oil: every descent burns oil you must buy, plus Dunn's gear, so money stays meaningful without a shop to run. A thing you don't need is never junk; it is a lamp tier you haven't bought yet, and a thing you can't carry today is still down there tomorrow.

## The second progression

Trust, kept by Pim in the Ledger. It rises with returns and with kept promises (Pim remembers what you said you'd bring back and whether you did) and never with coin. Ranks: Nobody, Finder, Keeper, Warden of the Hollow. Each rank restores one building and brings one family back to an empty house in Nock, and each newcomer arrives with a want that is a new system (the cart, the rope track, the sled, the memory jars). Trust decides what the home becomes: at Nobody it is a half-empty village with a hole in it; at Warden it is a full village whose mountain breathes slowly. A village that trusts you also tells you more: the Deep shelf, and what Orre lost down there, only open at Keeper.

## What breaks the calm

The Swallow. The mountain inhales: a low chime, the petals stream toward the Throat, lanterns swing, the ground tilts and everything loose slides toward the hole. For 40 seconds you scramble (this is the Help toy, kept and repurposed): catch the kettle, pin the basket, grab the goat, while villagers shout what matters to them. Whatever you didn't hold goes down, and someone's calm goes with it: Marit at a cold oven, Orre staring at the rope. This is the fast beat; the Hollow is the slow one, the opposite pace Dave found it needed. Swallows are scheduled at story moments and get bigger (hearthstone, then the bridge planks, then the bell, then a person), and between them the backlog of older losses in the Hollow means there is always a reason to go down without a chore list. The player half-caused the loss by what they failed to catch, which is why the return is theirs.

## Where accomplishment lands

The Putting Back, on the surface, in daylight. You come up with the thing, walk it to where it belongs, and place it: the oven lights and bread smell drifts across the terrace, the bell rings noon again, the bridge takes your weight. The owner says a line that remembers something you said (Pim told them, or they overheard), Pim crosses the Ledger entry out loud, and one lantern at the Throat lights, so the village visibly keeps score of calm restored. A smaller landing lives in the Hollow itself: the first time a new lamp tier carries you past the chamber that always went dark on you.

## The one-map conceit

The Hollow keeps everything anyone has ever lost, anywhere, stacked by age: last week's kettle near the top, a drowned lighthouse's lens a shelf down, things lost before anyone remembers at the bottom. One hole, one mountain, one village on its lip. New content is one prefab, one owner line and one shelf placement; a new chapter is a deeper shelf opened by a longer rope; travelers from other towns can arrive forever asking for things lost far away, and they are all down there. No new system is ever needed to add a thing, a creature, a letter, a song in a jar, or a person.

## Characters and requests

ORRE, winch-keeper, seventy, counts everything, lost her brother in the Great Swallow and runs the rope so nobody else goes. She introduces the descent: the bucket, the bell, the rope dial and the fail-state ('I haul up what's on the rope. Be on the rope.'). MARIT, baker, brisk, generous, physically unable to say thank you, wants the oven lit before cart day. She introduces the first Swallow and the Putting Back (the hearthstone). PIM, eleven, keeps the Ledger, remembers every word you say and quotes it back at the wrong moment, wants to be taken seriously and to write the last line. Pim introduces the Ledger, Trust ranks, the request board and memory as a currency. HESPER, tinker, ex-Finder who quit after a bad bet in the dark, cocky, charges extra, wants never to go down again and to be proven wrong about you. Hesper introduces the Bench: dismantling, parts, the lamp and pack tracks, later the rope and the sled. Later, DUNN, the cart trader, introduces coin, oil and selling, and every family that moves back introduces one more system by asking for something.

## Two lines

- Hesper: "You told me 'one more chamber' is what other people say. Then you came up with a lamp full of nothing and one wet sock. I wrote it down. I don't write things down."
- Hesper: "Stone to Marit, kettles to me, and the goat belongs to nobody, which makes it parts. Joke. Mostly. Now go tell Orre you're fine before she counts you twice."

## Later, in order

- Pim's Ledger board: several open losses at once, pick which to chase, Trust rank Finder, first family moves back.
- Hesper's full Bench: pack and rope tracks, the hook tool, and dismantling as the universal second sink.
- Dunn's cart: coin, oil per trip, selling parts, buying the storm lantern; cart day as the only clock on the surface.
- The Old shelf: second stratum of older, stranger things, lost animals that move, lamp-down glowmoss puzzles.
- Big Things: the sled, multi-trip hauls (bridge planks, the bell), Nock physically rebuilt piece by piece.
- Lost memories: small glass things; returning one changes what a character remembers and says (the Remember toy scaled up).
- The Deep shelf and lost people: Orre's brother, the end-game Swallow, Trust rank Warden.
- Travelers' losses: strangers from other towns arrive with requests for things lost far away; content forever, no new systems.

## Presentation

Low-poly 3D with a fixed three-quarter follow camera. On the surface it sits wide and warm; in the Hollow it drops lower and closer with the lamp as the only light (one OmniLight3D plus volumetric fog) so the lamp dial is felt as a shrinking circle, not a bar. Why: the designer already has a 3D pipeline (glTF kit, style contract, validator, a walkable Godot scene and character controller), grey boxes in 3D are literally boxes, light falloff is free in 3D and would cost a whole cave art pipeline in 2D, and A Short Hike proves a solo low-poly 3D game in this exact look. Chambers are hand-built scenes instantiated into shelf slots, not generated geometry.

## The kit

Adapted. All 27 pieces become Nock's upper terrace: the tea house is the Finder's house, the engawa the porch you start every morning on, the bridge spans to the Throat, the lanterns are the Trust lanterns that light one per return, the stone garden is the Putting Back yard, and the sakura petals are the Swallow's visual tell (they stream toward the hole). Added later, through the existing style contract and validator: about 12 surface pieces (winch frame, bucket, rope coil, bell, bakery oven front, bench shed, terrace walls, two house shells, ledger board, cart), roughly 3 Claude days, zero days now because the toy is grey boxes. The Hollow needs its own 10-12-piece rock and shelf kit later, about 3 days, which it would need under any concept. Cost of adapting rather than dropping: the setting stays an unplaced mountain-east village rather than Nordic, and the tea house stops being a tea house.

## The loop toy

Contents: 1 surface square (Throat, oven, bench shutter, three walkable terraces from the existing scene); 8 hand-built grey chambers on 2 shelves, 4 drawn and re-ordered per descent plus a landing; 6 lost things (hearthstone 3, kettle 1, lantern 1, letter 0, goat 2 that follows, bell-clapper 2) and 2 hollow-stuff (glowmoss, old iron); 3 characters (Orre, Marit, Pim, reusing Pim's dialogue memory), Hesper as a bench shutter with two lines; 1 upgrade track (lamp: 75, 110, 150 s) bought with glowmoss; dials fixed at rope 2 shelves and pack 4; 2 scripted Swallows (1:30 and after the first Putting Back); the dark fail-state with keep-one-by-touch; the Far sound hum and the Help scramble ported from the existing toys. Claude build: 5 days (day 1 threshold transition, lamp light and timer, chamber shuffle; day 2 items, pack, pickup, Putting Back with world change; day 3 Swallow scramble from the Help toy and the Ledger; day 4 fail-state, bench upgrade, Orre, Marit and Pim lines; day 5 tuning with the designer). The 20-minute question: after the dark takes a haul from the tester, do they go back down for it without being told to?

## Hypothesis line

I believe scramble, descend, bet, put back with grey boxes holds a confidant 20 minutes because every trip starts with a loss they watched happen and ends with a thing visibly back in its place; dead if not by 2026-11-07.

## Shared with Dave, and yours

Structurally shared, on purpose: a calm home above and a risky out below, a deliberate threshold, three dials each with an upgrade track, a fail-state that is a bet, two sinks so nothing wastes, a non-money progression that decides the home, a one-map conceit, and characters who hand you every system. What is yours: the trip is a response to a loss you watched and partly failed to prevent, so the fast beat (the Swallow) comes before the slow one and generates the requests, where Dave's fast beat comes after and consumes the haul. The goal is restoration, not accumulation: your pack empties into the village and the home grows from what you give back, not from what you buy. The fail-state defers loss instead of destroying it (the mountain keeps everything, so a lost haul is a deeper reason to return). Memory, not customers, is the social currency, and the home has no shop to run. The map's reshuffle is the same event as the calm-breaker: the mountain breathes, and that breath both takes the thing and rearranges where it fell.

## Three ways it fails

- The Putting Back lands as delivery, not restoration: placing the thing is a click with no weight. The toy must make every return change the world (light, smell, sound, a person's line that remembers you) or the loop is a fetch quest with a lamp.
- The Hollow reads as a dark corridor with a timer, which is a chore, not wonder. Every chamber needs one strange lost thing and one small physical question, and the shuffle must make 'where am I' without making a maze; if testers describe it as 'the cave bit' the concept is dead.
- The Swallow feels unfair or predictable: if the thing you cared about always goes, players feel punished rather than needed, and if it fires on a visible schedule it becomes a chore clock. Scope is the shadow risk: 3D chambers tempt generation; the rule is hand-built rooms re-ordered, never generated geometry.

## The judges

**Designer, 7/10.** The Swallow is the best calm-break of the six: the petals stop drifting and stream toward the Throat, you scramble for forty seconds, you watch the hearthstone go, and every descent is for something specific you half-failed to hold; keep-one-by-touch in the dark ('something that is trying to lick you') is the best fail-state writing in the batch, and Hesper the ex-Finder is a character you want to meet again. But it throws away the tea house as a tea house for a generic restore-the-village trope, the lamp at 75-200 seconds makes the out a dash rather than a dive (no room for hard-but-not-too-hard puzzles or wonder), and a Swallow on a visible schedule is exactly the chore clock the designer fears. Alive: the petals, the coin of daylight, one lantern lighting per return. Generic: families moving back, buildings restoring one per rank.

*Best idea in it:* The Swallow: the calm-break is a short scramble you half-fail at, so the loss is witnessed and partly yours, and the same breath that takes the thing re-stacks the chambers it fell into.

*The one change:* Make the Hollow a dive, not a dash: lamp tiers in minutes rather than seconds and three or four chambers per shelf with one small physical question each, so the slow half can carry exploration, puzzles and strange lost things instead of a sprint to a hum.

**Producer, 6.5/10.** The leanest toy (5 days, rope and pack fixed, one track variable) and the strongest emotional scoreboard: each Putting Back visibly restores Nock (the oven lights, the bell rings noon), keep-one-by-touch is the best-written fail-state of the six, and rope-as-leash means every reach upgrade raises the lamp's stakes. But the home is a village, not a tea house: twelve surface pieces, house shells, an oven, families moving back, and the brief admits the kit 'stops being a tea house', so the one finished asset loses its identity and the surface art bill is the largest here. The Swallow (ground tilts, loose things slide, a 40-second scramble) is a physics scene the risk list does not flag, and because Swallows are story-scheduled the home has no per-loop fast beat between them.

*Best idea in it:* The fail-state in the dark: you choose what to keep by touch ('something cold and round', 'something that is trying to lick you'), the mountain keeps the rest one shelf deeper and slightly changed, and Pim writes 'lost again, deeper' in the Ledger.

*The one change:* Shrink Nock to the kit: the tea house is the last standing house on the lip, villagers walk up to it, and families 'move back' into kit rooms rather than new house shells; that removes most of the surface art bill and keeps the tea house a tea house.
